I've gotten burned out on AWoGaF. I've been writing it since June 22, 2024, and I just don't have the energy to keep going. I had been in denial, but when I prayed in Adoration, I just felt God gently tell me I was burned out. So for the moment, here's a new project.

I don't know when I'll get back to AWoGaF, and I realize I left at an awkward time, but it is what it is. Until then, here's a different project.

I have a bunch of ideas for it but not much written. I don't have a blurb. I don't even have a name yet. It _is_ a LitRPG, but beyond that, I don't know. Discovery!

People you should know:

James Algol: Our hero, an ordinary Worker Transmitted to another world.\
Stella Cobol: James's forbidden Thinker girlfriend who, last he heard, was also going to be transmitted.\

The story so far:

Chapter 1: James is tried for the grievous sin of slapping a Perfect in the face, after the Perfect had attempted to take Stella. He is sentenced to Transmission.

Chapter 2: We learn more about the world. Stella tells James that she'll be Transmitted, too, but she learned something about the Destination. There are eight worlds to climb, but death sends you back to a random place on the first one. There's also some strange system there, one that makes no sense. Also, the Perfects know something about the Destination that Stella can't put into words. 

The first world is enormous, filled with millions of people. The two agree to meet up in the second world, so they can find each other.

Chapter 3: James heads to the Transmission center, where, strangely, the doctor rubs a very expensive nanobiotic healing solution on his arm. He attempts to escape, but they successfully Transmit him.

Chapter 4: James is Transmitted to a mysterious cave, where he discovers the world comes with no tutorial, and the interface is in a language he doesn't recognize. A terrifying giant enemy crab appears and kills him immediately.

Chapter 5: James respawns, and continues to explore the strange system. He discovers how to work his inventory, and realizes he might be able to craft a spear. After a battle with a snake, he discovers wounds don't draw blood, but simply turn the injured body part red. In the process of exploring further creepy stuff, he is summarily murdered by a troll.

Chapter 6: After endless respawns, James considers giving up, but remembers Stella in a flashback. He experiments with crafting but finds he is not very good at it. But his improvised knife allows him to fill his "experience" meter in a battle with a snake, before he falls unconscious

## Discovery

James wondered, when he floated in space again, whether it was all a cruel joke. Maybe there was no other worlds; maybe there was just constantly being murdered over and over again by the monsters. An eternal punishment, never to cease.

What had killed him, just as he was about to level up? Unless it was a cruel joke, and he just would suffer---No. No, Stella would not want him to give up. She would want him to find her. And then they could be together.

* * *

He woke with another massive headache, still in a cave. He felt incredibly alive, as if he had just been born.

He checked his inventory, to find it empty. Yes, he had died.

How? What killed him? Poison?

James got up, brushed himself off, and wondered why he always respawned with a hospital gown. Or why every death, he felt so alive. At least he had gotten a bare idea of what to do: knap rocks, kill monsters, find those yellow pulp plants, and maybe, somehow, find something to be used as a stick. He knew he could create a spear, he just needed to---

He froze, because he heard not a slither nor a skitter, but voices. Human voices. Chatting away conversationally. "Yeah, I told him he was too dumb to make it. You'd think a Thinker would---"

James couldn't hold it in any more. "Over here!" he shouted, the echo bouncing off the distant tunnels.

He heard the voices speed up. "*Another* newt? Today must be our lucky day. Hey! Newt! Where are you?"

"I don't know!" James called out. "I'm in a room, where---"

Footsteps approached, and a party of humans---humans!---with mismatched armor stepped inside. "Hey, how's it going?" the young man who had spoken earlier said, with Worker-dark hair. "You a newt?"

"A what?" James asked.

"Newly Transmitted," a young woman Thinker by him explained.

"I've been murdered by monsters for the last few... weeks? Months? I don't even know," James admitted.

"Welcome to the Underneath," the leader said. "This way. We'll take care of you."

For a brief moment James wondered if it was a trick.

"I know. You're not sure if we're going to torture you or feed you to a monster, right?"

"Drake, _please,_" the Thinker said. "He's suffered enough without your humor."

"I'll come," James said, and followed them.

The party continued to banter and bicker as they took seemingly random turns through the caves. James noticed that while they didn't have a map, they did make marks on the walls. And, well, they were going upwards.

"Are we heading to the surface?" James asked.

"Maybe in a few months," Drake said. "Oh, you're a newt. The Surface is the next world up. We're in the Underneath."

"Why are we going up, though?" James asked.

"You didn't realize that that's where the monsters are less dangerous?" Drake asked.

James hit his face, and had to cancel out of the screens.

"Hey, chill," the Thinker said. "We were all newts once."

"Who are you?" James asked.

"Kate," the Thinker said. "Oh, we're part of the Dragon Trog Tribe. You're welcome to stay with us."

"I will," James said. "Please."

They came to larger and larger tunnels until they came at last to one massive cavern. A large campsite stood inside, with a palisade complete with gates and guards. They came to one. "Hey, Drake," one of the guards said. "Got a second newt today?"

"I know!" Drake said. "Welcome to our camp. We just need you to come to the quartermaster, and then you can go on your way."

"Uh, OK," James said, and headed with them into the camp.

## Dragon Trogdolyte Tribe

The inside of the camp showed simple structures, little more than tents, but it was crowded. James felt himself relax as he saw the various members of the Tribe sit around and joke. Many were cooking at campfires, while others seemed to be sparring. James did some math in his head and came up with a community of a few hundred people in this camp.

One large tent apparently held the quartermaster, as well as loads of crates. The older man, sitting on the rock floor was writing in a book. On seeing James, the quartermaster smiled. "Ah, a newt? Ready to leave your days of constant dying behind?"

"Yes, please," James said.

"Not much loot," Drake interrupted. He and the quartermaster opened up their inventories and touched them together. James watched, fascinated, as the quartermaster took some pieces of meat and yellow and blue bulbs. The quartermaster then wrote in a book. "See you," Drake said to James, then he and his party headed out.

"Um, hi," James said. "I'm James."

"I'm Quartermaster Boris," the older man said. "Welcome to the Dragon Trogdolyte Tribe. You're welcome to stay with us, if you'd like, but we'll put you to work."

"Anything is better than dying," James said.

"A good attitude. Now, I'll provide you a standard kit, but to be clear, it's loaned, not given. You'll have negative credit for a bit."

"...Negative?" James asked, concerned.

"Oh, we're not loan sharks. We just can't hand out freebies. You can take your time repaying. And the bluefruit stew is free. So, still want your standard kit?"

"Yes, sir," James said, relaxing. He still felt nervous around these people, but even if they did have some kind of elaborate scam going, he was still better off than trying to make it by himself in the depths of the Underneath.

The quartermaster whistled to himself, digging through a crate. "Let me transfer," he said.

James opened his inventory, and merged with the quartermaster as he had seen Drake do. The quartermaster transferred a shortsword, a spear, a complete set of clothing with sandals, a complete set of armor, and a ring. "We won't replace any of it if you lose it," the quartermaster said. "We had a string of petty thefts recently, but it stopped once we found the culprit. Just hold on to your items, and put them in your inventory if you're not using them."

"Yes, sir," James said. "How much will this cost?"

"One hundred credits," the quartermaster said, writing a number---in blessedly familiar Arabic numerals, on a page in his book. "For reference, a nice meal is a credit, and a new sword is about ten credits. You can easily make it back with a trip or two, but until you know how to use your items, you're going to just be doing guard duty."

"Will I be assigned...?" James asked.

"Everyone has to do _something_, but we don't object to you picking whatever job you can do. We don't respect the Scale down here. For the moment, be on guard duty, and train whenever you can."

"Where do I sleep?" James asked.

"Just head over to the men's dormatory tent and find someone who doesn't snore to share a blanket with. Or, if you can't, just sleep."

"...OK. And food?"

"The bluefruit stew is by the big fire. Just show up at mealtime and it'll be waiting."

"What if I get hurt?"

"Rest will cure it, as long as you don't go over your wound meter."

"My what?"

"The rightmost bar on your stats screen."

James opened it up. That bar could only be the same one he thought had been experience. _"That's_ a wound meter?"

"Yes, as you are injured it will go up as the system repairs your body. If it goes too high you die. So if you're badly hurt, head to the hospital tent. It will cost you, but we'll always treat you."

James realized how little he knew about any of the systems. That 'experience' bar had grown as he had gotten hurt, and eventually killed him.

"Also, we have a few rules here," the quartermaster said. "No fighting. No stealing. No cheating on your partner. Everyone must work. All loot must go to me for proper credit. And obey the words of the Tribe Leader or Tribe Officers without question in combat or emergency settings. That's it."

"You seem to be prepared for people joining and leaving all the time," James said.

"They do. That's the nature of being a Trog Tribe."

"What _is_ a Trog Tribe?"

"We travel the Underneath in a large nomadic group, as opposed to the Holds, which stay in place, or the Wanderers, who travel from Hold to Hold in small groups. If you want to switch from one to another, you can. We're all in this together, you see."

James felt his whole body relax of a tension he didn't realize he had been carrying. He had expected a bunch of competing groups fighting each other for resources. But in fact it seemed that the groups didn't fight each other, or at least had some kind of truce. It wasn't every man for himself. It was every man for every man.

"Head around the camp and see what you'd like," the quartermaster advised. "It's up to you what you want out of life, after all."

"Doesn't everyone want to get out?"

The quartermaster shook his head. "Many do at first. But most of them give up soon enough."

## Camp

He stepped out into the camp. Was this going to be his new home? Better get used to it, then.

"Hey," a Trog man said, coming up to him. "The clothing here is weird. Let me help you put in on."

"Uh, OK?" James said. But a few minutes later, he wasn't arguing. The clothing they had given him made no sense. The pants had one leg drastically shorter than the other, while the shirt was more like a strange tunic. Something between a cloak or a cape draped over his other shoulder than the short leg. Most important of all, he was told, were the headbands and armbands, that showed the red dragon of their tribe.

"Who designed these clothes?" James asked.

"No norking clue," the Trog said. "This is more or less what we find in jars."

"Jars?"

"Yeah, the chests down here come in these weird circular jars. At least the armor makes sense. Anyway, see you around.

James walked out in admittedly comfortable clothing, with equipment in his inventory and the promise of food, and more importantly, the promise of not being repeatedly murdered by monsters.

He hoped Stella had also been picked up by a Trog Tribe, but he thought it inconceivably unlikely that she was in this camp. He asked around anyway.

He did find Drake's party warming themselves by a fire. They opened up a space for him. "How's it going, newt?" Drake asked.

"My name is James," James protested.

"Fine. How's it going?"

"He's just teasing you," Kate said.

"Can... you explain this place? Or the system?"

"Nope. But we have some basic ideas."

"I've figured out the inventory screen, sort of," James said. "The rest I don't really understand."

"Here," Kate said, and touched her nose. "The first is inventory, as you figured out." She touched her nose again. "The second is a weird clock astrology thing. We're not sure how it works, but you can stick an item in that little box and something may or may not happen."

"How do you know?" James asked.

"We don't. And sometimes you stick something in and it gives it a really nasty curse, so the general rule is not to mess with it unless you have insane amounts of wealth to waste."

"OK," James said. "What about the next screen?"

Kate touched her nose. "We have no idea. No one's ever managed to figure this one out."

"I see," James said, feeling deflated.

Kate went to the next screen. "This is stats, although we're not sure what all of them do. It seems like when you do things, bars go up, and if they're up enough, you gain... something. We're not sure how, or even what the stats do. But it's clear enough that having bigger numbers is better, and we _have_ figured out the number system."

"Talk to our lorekeeper," Drake suggested. "You can learn more about what little we know."

"Also, that red bar at the end is very dangerous," Kate added.

"I thought it was experience," James admitted.

They laughed, but not in mockery. 

"And then there's the thumb trees," Kate said, and opened her last screen.

James looked, puzzled, at a different set of trees. Kate's black tree was much smaller than James, and her white tree slightly bigger.

"Everyone has a different explanation for the trees," Drake said, shaking his head. "Some people say they're a kind of experience. Others, a kind of history of your actions. No one can explain why they grow and shrink. No one can explain what they mean. No one has found any way to interact with them, either."

"I think it's experience," Kate confided. "Because it sticks with you between respawns."

"Wait, it _does?_" James asked.

"Yes, it's weird that way. No one's ever explained why _that_ of all things does it. And one thing we've all noticed is that killing things tends to grow your trees, which is _probably_ a good sign."

"I heard---" a huge Worker man rumbled.

"Oh, not this theory again," another Worker partymate muttered.

"I told you, I heard it from a friend."

"---who heard it from a friend---" Drake mocked.

"Who said that a Perfect said it was a kind of karma."

"A Perfect?" James asked. "How?"

"I know, right?" Drake said.

"It's not impossible," Kate said. "I mean, maybe some Perfect wanted to go on an adventure. He'd be a total idiot, though. I know what _I'd_ do to a Perfect if I had the chance." There was sheer venom in her voice.

"The problem with Gus's theory," Drake started.

"It's not a theory!" Gus protested.

"The problem with Gus's _idea_ is that it makes no sense what actions grow or shrink your trees. Like, killing another human causes either to skyrocket, sometimes both, and there's no explanation why or how. And sometimes they just grow or shrink on their own!"

"People kill each other down here?" James asked.

A quiet fell on the camp. A thin Thinker partymate shook her head. "It happens, but it's very rare. Every now and then the drama in a Trog Tribe---and believe me, there's _always_ drama---goes too far, and they have a fight amongst themselves. Usually they just split rather than fight it out. Or a Tribe goes rogue and start attacking Holds, and the other Tribes have to hunt them down. But there's just no point. We're all in danger at all times. So we all need to stick together."

"I see," James said. Yes, it made sense. They had made a much better world without the Perfects. In fact, chances were everyone here had his own bad run-in with a Perfect.

"Hey," Drake said. "You got injured earlier? On your arm?"

James lifted up his arm to see a strange mark on it, like a scrape that had never healed. "Yeah," he said. "I'd scratched myself accidentally before coming here."

"It'll never heal. Ever," Drake said. "But it doesn't look bad."

"Let me see," Kate said, and reached over to examine his arm. "Yeah. You'll be fine."

"They put some nanoheal on before they sent me."

"Glack, _really?"_ Drake asked. "Why can't they just hand it out Back There?"

"I also had a nasty cut, and they put nanoheal on it," Gus confided. "Still left a bit of a mark."

"Why?" James asked. "Why are they so generous about _this_ of all things?"

"Nork if I know," Drake said.
