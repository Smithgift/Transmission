## Breakdown

James gripped his spear, but all around, no monsters showed. The Sun, or whatever the nurck the Oru called their star, still merrily shone above. So what else?

"Now what?" Ralph muttered, shaking his head.

The other mercenaries were little more happy, but the captain bellowed over all of them. "One of the haulers broke. We're trying to repair it."

"Just leave it behind!" a mercenary shouted what James felt.

"That's not an option! Now shut up and stay on guard."

The merceneries grumbled and stood around. "Great," James said. "Just nurcking great." He looked up. The 'Sun' would not be merrily shining much longer, or at least it would go down over the canyon walls. "Why _can't_ they just leave it behind? Profit?"

"No," Ralph said. "We're carrying vital supplies. Without that hauler, Ironspire would run out of wood for fires or just plain food."

"Can the Perfects sabotage machinery?" Stella asked Lucky with concern.

She shrugged. "Probably not. There's no option to, but it's remotely possible that high _hamartia_ makes machines break."

"I don't know if they needed that," Violet said. "If it's that particular hauler that got hit, maybe something internal got damaged."

"It's possible," Stella said. "I'd think it would have broken earlier."

"Could have been something like an oil leak or some gear worn away," James mused. Then he thought about it. He might not know about machines here, but he did know things about machines in general, and Stella was an expert. "Hey, Stella, let's see if we can help."

She nodded, and they squeezed themselves past mercenaries back through the line of haulers.

* * *

A crowd of mercenaries and merchants surrounded the crowd of mechanics who in turn surrounded the left rear of the machine---right next to the dent. James had wondered briefly if they would have to argue with them to let Stella and he look at the machine. "Excuse me," Stella called. "I was an engineer with Pony Polymechanics---"

One Worker man looked up from the mass of parts. _"Stella?"_

"Paul! I was wondering where you had ended up!"

"See if you can figure what the nurck happened." He motioned her through the crowd. James followed, no one objecting.

Paul OCaml had been one of James' coworkers, who had gotten Transmitted over an argument with a superior. He had the charisma and social skills of a vat-grown turnip, but James had to admit, Paul was good with machines.

James also had to admit he had little idea of what he was looking at, but it seemed mostly mechanical parts. Strangely enough, not a single part seemed even slightly worn or rusty. That was one of the strange things about the Tower of Worlds: nothing every wore out or got damaged unless you deliberately did something to it.

"Nothing seems wrong," Paul explained. "But it's not working."

"Power?" Stella asked.

"Checked that. Generator seems fine."

"I mean the junction."

"Checked---oh, dung, did we check the valve?"

James wondered what a "valve" was. Another mechanic twisted a knob on a pipe and a blue fluid dripped out before flashing into a crack of lightning.

"A leak. Knew it had to be something nurcking stupid," Paul muttered.

"What can we do?" the young merchant from before called.

"Here? Nothing. No sane person would try to fix a live electrohydraulics pipe. Need to drain that dung before you get fried. And there's nowhere to drain it _to!_"

"We can't just leave a fifth of our supplies---"

"---Told you, we can't work on this. It'd kill someone."

"Can't you just take the supplies off this hauler and put it on the others?" Stella asked. "There's plenty of room."

Everyone paused and looked at her.

"That's obvious," the young merchant said. "Do it. We'll have to leave the hauler behind."

"Agreed." The captain of the guard started bellowing orders and everyone, merchant, mercenary, and mechanic, hurried to help.

* * *

Unloading and reloading took hours, and not before long, only the dimming blue sky remained of the day. The work had to be done via torchlight.

James and Stella hefted up a bundle of wood tied with an orange-green rope onto a pile in the front hauler. Another strange thing: on Earth, Stella's slight build had needed help with even medium-sized weights, but with all their increased stats, she could lift them easily.

Someone else overloaded a different pile and but a bundle of wood fell off the side of the hauler and crashed. "Leave it!" the captain barked. "We need to get moving."

James saw Lucky grab a piece of wood and run off down the canyon walls. James followed. "Lucky? What are you---?"

Lucky set the wood down, pulled out her tinderbox from the inventory, and set the piece of wood on fire. Then she threw a scroll into it, blazing yellow. _"Alari alui paru hara Oru."_ she chanted. With a yellow flash, a massive scroll appeared in her hand. She unrolled it, looked at it and trembled.

"Lucky, what?" James said, and trailed off when he saw the contents.

Whatever spell Lucky had just cast had to be some kind of mapping spell, because James saw a detailed topological map of the canyon and everything nearby. James could see they were not that far from the fortified buildings that must be Ironspire, but closer than Ironspire, atop the canyon was a large group of colored symbols in a circle. "What are those?"

"That's the Oru character for 'Oru'," Lucky said.

"There's _Oru_ here?" James asked.

"Humans close enough to Oru for the spell to think they are. And if those aren't a large Free patrol that decided to hang out in the mountains for no reason..."

"Those are Slavers," James finished.
