## Color Item

"A powerful enchanted ring?" James asked.

"Even better!" Stella said, her voice the near squeal it could be when really excited.

"It's a color item," Lucky explained.

"A what?"

Lucky stood up, pointed at a distant boulder, and chanted in Oru. A flame spear shot out and crashed into it, spewing bits of red-hot sand and ash everywhere. "See?"

"Uh..."

"That ring always gives her access to red magic," Stella explained. "In fact, the red color it's radiating has thrown off all our enchantment attempts."

"Yeah, the rest of you might be able to use red magic if you're close enough to me," Lucky said. 

James's mind whirled. This changed everything. Lucky could hammer everything in sight with red spells, and as long as she didn't overuse her magic and fill that meter, she could just keep doing it. No need to buy more darts. No need to conserve scrolls---red scrolls, at least. Just point, chant, and blow up. "Where did you _find_ this?" James asked.

"No idea," Lucky said. "It must have been in one of the random chests. Anyway, now that we know we're dealing with red everywhere, we _should_ be able to compensate and enchant stuff... as long as it's red enchantments."

"We'll need to go in for more equipment," Violet said. "None of this stuff is usable, alas." 

James looked at the pile of cool-looking but now essentially worthless items and felt the grief of a gamer who has just lost plenty of perfectly good loot.

"I also think..." Stella trailed off.

"Go on," James encouraged.

"We might want to slow down a little. Maybe if we did only a few rooms a day as opposed to trying to clear whole dungeons in one adventure. Now that Lucky can use magic freely, our biggest resource is her magic meter."

"Stella, are you sure?" Lucky asked. "You'll be attacked every night."

"I know, but... we have to look at the bigger picture."

"Stella," James said firmly. "You're important, too."

"I know, but it's my fault I'm so scared of spiders."

"It's the fault of that dungpit Perfect who keeps sending them. That Vulkan jerk is probably laughing his---"

"---Wait," Violet interrupted.

They turned to her.

"Lucky, your benefactors can send monsters, too, yeah?"

"Of course," Lucky said. "They have access to all the same features we---every Perfect---does."

"So why not ask them to send a non-spider monster at the beginning of the night? Then the other Perfects can't send another attack that night, right? We've never been attacked twice in the same night."

"Yeah, the cooldown does last that long," Lucky said. "It should work."

"Hear that, Lucky's friends!" James shouted upwards. "Give Stella a break!"

"James!" Lucky snapped. "Don't just shout that."

"Fine," he said. "But we have a solution now, and we _can_ go slow."

"We'll try it, at least," Lucky said. "I don't have control over my benefactors, and I don't know what resources they'll have available at any given time. But I would appreciate it if they did, yes."

Ralph looked up. "There's still some time left today. Let's do some dungeon crawling, now that Lucky's super-powered."

* * *

"Super-powered" was right. Back in the mines, they found another dungeon. No warning signs next to it: it looked newly spawned.

"'Should a fool venture cowardice in here let him beware that the curse of shadows would be dealt unto him.'" Lucky read with relief. "So much simpler." 

"But what counts as 'cowardice?''" James asked.

"Letting go of a weapon. It's the same curse as the Underneath one. And 'shadows' is just blindness."

"A whole nurcking load easier than the last one," Ralph said. They formed up and went inside.

Ralph was right twice: not only was the curse so much easier to deal with, but Lucky's new powers made the monsters almost trivial. What made it even more trivial was how little Lucky's magic meter went up with a lethal fire spear. The party had been reduced to tanks for Lucky's DPS.

Did the Oru even think in terms of tanks, DPS, and healer? James wondered. As Lucky had said, they had no shared ludic touchpoints. Did Oru game designers create the same kind of systems that lead to that kind of thinking among Oru gamers? Or was that unique to Earth, and the Oru had a completely different definition of what a MMORPG party should be? Did the Oru reinvent (preinvent?) Tetris? Or did they conceive of entire new genres of games that were now lost to time?

Admittedly, James had to wish that he wasn't playing _this_ Oru game. But then again, if it hadn't been for the Tower of Worlds, the Perfects would have been executing people left and right.

"James," Stella said, bringing him back to the present.

"Sorry," he mumbled, and returned to focus.

After the sixth room, Lucky wanted to call it quits. "We should get back. It's got to be late, and we have to retrace our steps. We did get plenty of loot, though. Maybe a bit too much." She added after they struggled to find room for the remaining items they'd gotten from the jar. They already had thrown out some cheap supplies on the theory they'd be able to sell the loot for far more and rebuy them.

James didn't argue. He was used to dungeons, particularly underground ones, being deadly. Things had gone _far_ too smoothly for comfort.

* * *

When they left the mineshaft, Ralph swore at the setting sun. "We should have stopped a room earlier."

"What's the big deal?" Paul asked, coming up beside them. "You'll have plenty of time to get back to Ironspire."

Yeah, either Paul had zero social skills, or he was stalking Stella. The first was definitely true, in any case.

"Let's get moving then," Lucky said, and they marched on.

"Where are you staying, by the way?" he called after them.

"None of your business!" James shouted back.

"Just trying to help!"

"Ignore him," Stella told James, but James decided immediately to not do so. Twice was a coincidence. Three times was too far.

"Let's take a right," Ralph said. "We'll go around the outside of Ironspire, since I don't think we can get through in time."

"We don't have any wood," Stella said. "We left it behind---"

"Yeah, we _have_ to go to Ironspire," Violet said.

"Then let's nurcking hurry!"

* * *

They didn't nurcking hurry fast enough. By the time they reached the mine-side entrance to Ironspire, the guards were already getting ready to lower the porticulis. "Hey!" one shouted. "Get in!"

"Lucky, what do we---" James started.

"Don't just stand there! Get! In!"

"Listen, it's complicated, but---" James tried.

A guard glared. "Are you that party that keeps coming in and out at weird times?"

"What does it matter?" Lucky shouted. "We're Free citizens."

"Yeah, and it's nurcking suspicious. Get the nurck in already!"

"Lucky, I don't think we have a choice," Stella murmured.

"...Fine," Lucky said. They went through the gate, not without further hostile stares from the guards.

"Now what?" James asked, his stomach dropping.

"Now we play it by ear," Lucky said. 

"You know, it's only a theory that the Perfects would send a Titan if we stayed in a city," Violet said, in a tone that didn't sound like even she believed it.

"If Vulkan Lisp is after us, he could afford to send a Titan," Lucky said. "And the more we stress about it, the more likely he's going to send one. Unless he wants to play mindgames, which is entirely him."

"Yeah, go nurck yourself," James said to the sky. Then kissed Stella, who held the kiss for a deliberately long time.

They let go. "Let's go stay in an inn, at least," Stella said. "And we can discuss what to do."

* * *

They ended up paying for one room, ground-floor as Lucky insisted. ("Better to escape if a Titan hits.") There was only one bed, but James knew none of them were sleeping. He didn't even bother to take off his armor.

"I can't think of any way to leave the city now," Ralph said. "If a Titan _does_ come, what do we do?"

"Run?" Lucky suggested. "I don't think Ironspire is large enough to take on a Titan, but it's likely got enough _hamartia_ to make one spawn. And if not, Vulkan can always spread the hate around prior to pressing the button."

"City's surrounded by walls," Ralph said, shaking his head. "They _might_ open one of the porticulis for people to flee, but chances are they'll leave them closed against the additional monsters a Titan can summon."

"We can't climb the walls?" James asked, his shoulders dropping.

"We might survive the fall, but they're going to be heavily guarded atop, and we might not get through. 'cept the guards will probably be running to defend whatever wall is standing against the Titan." Ralph scratched his chin.

"Not that any of these walls would last a few seconds longer than it takes the Titan to get to them," Lucky mused.

"Titans are slow?" James asked.

"The Eyedra and Shimmera are pretty slow. It's just all the stuff they summon isn't. And the Great Wyrm can fly."

"Could we help fight it?" Stella asked. "I mean, we _are_ trying to get key fragments."

"Heh," Lucky said. "The irony. If we beat the awful odds and kill it, we're a third of the way there."

"Hear that, Vulkan, you lying dungpit?" James asked out loud. "If you send a Titan, you're just playing into our hands."

Nervous chuckles spread around the room, which James knew ruined the taunt. But maybe it still got under that jerk's skin.

"Here's a thought," Violet said. "If it smashes the walls, could we go around it once it attacks the city?"

"Depends on the Titan," Lucky said. "If it's the Eyedra, then it'd be barely possible. Shimmera would blast us with magic if we got close. And the Great Wyrm can fly."

"And now the Perfects aren't going to send us an Eyedra," James said.

"No, they can't choose," Lucky said. "It sends a random one."

"OK, so if it's the Eyedra, we try to escape, if it's the other two we fight and hope for the best," Violet said.

"Yeah. Bad odds." Lucky sighed. "But that's all we've got."

"I'm sorry," Stella said. "I should have insisted---"

Lucky grabbed Stella's hand in hers. They were silent for a moment, then let go.

"Yeah," Stella said. "Bad odds are better than no odds."

James was baffled, but Stella seemed much more reassured. "Anyway," James said. "This is crazy. The one night when Stella could get a perfect night's rest is the one night we all stay up because---"

A long, deafening-loud, HISS overpowered his words, bouncing off the canyon walls and echoing back.
