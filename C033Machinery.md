## Machinery 

They arrived in Northwood City just before nightfall. As they had been told, their IDs were welcome there, and the guards hurried them in before they closed the portcullis for the night.

The fire pits everywhere acted as kinds of streetlights, illuminating the darkened streets as they looked for a inn. To keep monsters away, but they weren't Lucky's special logs. "Hey, Lucky," James whispered. "Are we really safe from monsters in town?"

Lucky shook her head. "Probably not. They're not dealing with spawns because monsters can only spawn in sufficient darkness, but outside the walls, they'd be free to spawn and try to attack the walls. Or they may be burning some incense logs by accident. Or their _hamartia_ is low enough a regular fire will keep them at bay. Ours isn't."

James didn't know what to add to that.

* * *

They found an inn, and Lucky managed to bargain with the innkeeper with some materials rare even on the Surface. They all shared a room, and Lucky ended up on watch they mutually agreed to set.

James woke up the next morning to found he had, for the first time in he-didn't-know-how-long he had a decent sleep. Everyone else was already down at the table eating. Lucky passed him an untouched plate of bread. "Thanks," he said.

"It's for _arete_," she whispered.

James felt strange, but bit into the bread to find it actually tasted really good.

"So we were saying we need to find better gear," Ralph said. "They do have rooms in certain hills, like in the Underneath, but that's rare. Most of the good stuff comes from either monster drops or resource nodes. We might find jobs as low-level guards for a caravan to a mining outpost, or even _at_ an outpost."

"But we were wondering what you were going to do," Violet added.

"...What do you mean?" James asked.

"Are you going to stay around here? Or keep searching for your girlfriend?"

James didn't know how to answer that.

"We still have some rare materials," Ralph said. "We can take a bit of a break as we figure out what to do next."

"Yeah," Lucky mumbled.

"What's wrong?" Violet asked.

Lucky shook her head. "Nothing I want to talk about here."

"OK," Violet said.

"How about we go looking around for jobs?" Ralph suggested. "We should be safe in a settlement this size, and if we get split up, we'll agree to meet up back here."

"Sounds good," James said.

"All in favor?"

They all raised their hands.

* * *

James went down the streets, finding himself strangely lonely without his party. But in truth, _could_ he stay with them? He needed to find Stella, but if Stella wasn't already on the Surface, it meant he had to stay behind.

But Stella was more important. They all would sincerely understand that. And maybe the party would have less tension if he didn't keep accidentally setting off Lucky.

After accosting some other Transmitted, he found his way to the squat building where they tried to reconnect people. Stepping through the wooden doors, he saw rows and rows and rows of filing cabinets. He didn't know if that was a good sign or a bad sign. No line, so he just went up to clerk. "Hey, I'm looking for someone."

"Just fill out this form," she said, and pulled out what appeared to be a form reproduced by printing press. "Then come back each week."

"What do you mean 'each week?'" James asked, his stomach sinking.

The clerk gestured around. "We have a lot of people looking for others. It will take some time to search through our records. But please, fill it out. Perhaps the person you're looking for is already looking for."

That was a comforting thought. He quickly filled out the form.

* * *

But back on the streets of Northwood City, he realized he was in the same boat. Unless he would come back every week between caravan trips or whatever the rest of the party wanted to do, he'd have to leave them.

He passed by a large banner in front of a huge smoothstone building that rumbled from the inside: "Now hiring---Worldclimbers welcome!" He shrugged to himself and stepped inside.

Then covered his eyes. This must have been one of the factories that everyone had talked about: rows upon rows of multistory machines crushed rocks, cast ingots, and stamped plates into sword blades, among the tasks he could even recognize. A boss-looking man in what came the closest to business causal in the Tower of Worlds came up to him with a gleam in my eye. "Welcome! Looking for a job?"

"Erm, potentially?"

The boss's gleam spread to his smile, as if James had already agreed. "Let me show you around this place."

James could only marvel at the scale of the place, as well as try to resist the pounding noise when the boss insisted on showing up the machinery. They made both finished products and raw ingots, depending on what orders they had gotten. Right now it was a mix of enchanted blades and those ingots. "Enchanted?" James yelled.

"Yes, enchanted!"

Apparently the creators of the Tower of Worlds had different opinions on how metallurgy worked, for the machines sometimes purified, infused, or enchanted the materials they processed. James had briefly worked in one of the steelworks on Earth, so he knew that even the parts of this process that vaguely resembled real ones still were bizarrely different.

The boss took it in stride. "I know, how does shoving a item through a machine make it enchanted? Beats me, but we can use it."

"How good is it afterwards?"

"Probably enough to one-shot anything in the Underneath!"

James didn't know if it was a boast, but quite possibly not. There was just no way to go back down with these weapons. As they continued to travel through the factory into blissfully more quiet areas, James wondered if it would be a bad life to stop here and wait for Stella. He was good with machines, and even if these machines had little to do with the real world, they still were machines.

"This is the machine shop," The boss stopped on a platform overlooking a mess of parts and smaller machines. "We build and repair the machines here." 

James walked to the edge of the platform and looked at the workshop below to see a mix of Workers, Thinkers, and even a Watcher working away at machines. Then his jaw hit the floor.

"Stella?"
