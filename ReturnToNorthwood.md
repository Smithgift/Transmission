## Return to Northwood

They walked back in quiet. Lucky seemed to be in one of her depressed moods again, and despite her and Stella looking at each other a lot, not much happened.

"Hey!" the guard at the gate cheerfully said. "Ritual go well?"

James was baffled for a moment, before he remembered. "We all survived, and that's the best you can ask for."

"Things just happen to people in secret cults, you know," Lucky said.

The guards laughed and let them in.

Despite their conversation last night, Northwood City seemed the same as ever. James almost wanted to shout in random people's ears that they were part Oru, or how to learn spells, but he knew that without Lucky's proof, no one would believe him. And he didn't know why Lucky hadn't told everyone else---no, it had been because she had suffered so much. And telling others would probably hurt Lucky, too.

He glanced over her, to see her unsettled with the sort of stress that never ended. If he could just get them all to the fourth world, then even Lucky could rest. But at any moment, the Perfects could try to destroy them.

No, they couldn't. Even they had limits, given by the Oru. But were the Oru also sadists, in creating this place? No. Somehow, the thought of an entire alien civilization enjoying tormenting people was too much to bear. But as for _what_ this place was, he didn't know which of Lucky's theories was more comfortable.

"Oh, no," Stella said, pointing ahead to the angry crowd around the bank. "That's not good."

"It's not," James agreed. They approached the edge.

"I want my nurcking money!" shouted a protestor.

James tapped one of the nearby ones. "What's going on?"

"The bank had a run last night," the close protestor said. "They said the manager fled with the money last night, and they ran out of the rest."

"Nurck," Ralph said.

"I know!" a Thinker woman said. "Nurck them all!"

"We can't stick around to try and sort this out," Lucky said.

"Agreed," James said. They all headed away, Stella not without an upset look backwards.

"Could this have been..." Stella asked.

"No, regular people are just as capable of being dungpits," Lucky said. "Those up there aren't omnipotent."

Somehow, that felt better in a strange way. "Where next?" James asked.

"I still need to tell my boss I'm leaving," Stella said. "He's been good to me."

"And after that?" James asked.

"We did find a caravan was leaving today," Ralph said. "We were planning to leave with them before... well, before all this."

"They _should_ be safe," Lucky whispered. "But we'll probably be the target of a lot of 'random' monster attacks."

"Titans, though?" Stella asked.

"Nope. And that's the only reason we should join them. They'll get us closer to the dungeon hills."

"There's no reason you three need to come with us to say goodbye to my boss," Stella said.

Lucky shrugged. "Just meet up at the north gate where all the people are. We have plenty of time... _now._"

* * *

Stella's boss plead with her to stay, and then offered all manner of bonuses. "We need you!" he said at last.

"I'm sorry, but I need to worldclimb now," Stella said.

"This isn't about the bank run, is it?" the manager asked.

"No, although that was bad. I just can't stay any longer."

The manager almost physically dropped. "Well, if you change your mind, you'll always be welcome here."

"Thanks," Stella said. The two walked out.

"I actually feel bad about this," James admitted, once safely outside. In truth, he had never left a group in the Underneath where the group both survived and felt bad about him leaving.

"I do, too," Stella said. "Goodbyes are always hard." She turned to James. "I wanted to ask this when we were by ourselves. Is Lucky... OK?"

"I'm not sure what you mean by 'OK'," James said. "Probably not."

"She just seems so tormented to me."

"People tormented her," James said. "The only way she could escape is by being Lucky."

"I know, but..." She looked as if to say one thing more, but decided against it. "Hopefully we can get her to the fourth world."

"Us, too," James insisted.

"Yes, but if we die, it's not going to be as bad for us as her."

James didn't know how to answer that.

* * *

The caravan carried bulk raw material from Ironspire, a mining outpost in the no-mans-land between Slaver and Free cities, and also brought food and other supplies back. Because it was in that no-mans-land, mercenary contracts were insanely lucrative, as well as willing to take anyone.

This did not mean equally paid, however. James noted many of different levels of gear hopping on and off the giant wheeled machines. The quartermaster looked them over and said "Fifty freedollars a day for each of you."

"One hundred," Lucky said.

The quartermaster looked almost offended that an Unwanted would insist on raising the price. "Why?"

"We took on a Guardian with just our party," Lucky said. "We were elite among the elite in the Underneath."

"I mean, I wasn't there---" Stella started, but James squeezed her hand. She frowned.

"I saw battle at Seven Fords," Ralph said.

"Which division?" the quartermaster asked, suddenly suspicious.

"Greenwald."

"Nurck, you guys fought to the end. I can't just increase... you know what?" he said. "I'm going to put you at seventy. Most I can do without talking to the boss."

"We'll do seventy," Lucky said.

The quartermaster took down their names, and they went to the machines themselves. "What happened at Seven Fords?" James asked.

"I've been asking," Ralph said. "Tactical victory for the slavers, but strategic defeat, since they took so many causalties they couldn't advance. And that's all I'm going to say about it."

James didn't press. Even if no one really died, he wondered if anyone could truly get used to violence in the Tower of Worlds.

Stella was meanwhile explaining to Lucky about the vehicles, who listened in genuine interest. "...can't use turrets, because they take too much power and without stablizers they'd be completely inaccurate while moving."

"Why can't they just stop and fire?" Violet asked before James could.

"Again, power," Stella said. "The only efficient source they can carry with them are whiterock generators, and maybe they'd get a shot or two off if they had big enough capacitors. Nothing enough to stop a concerted attack on foot."

"Do tanks exist, then?" James asked.

"Landships, yeah. But they're _slow._ We were working on plates for their armor a few weeks ago."

"The Transmitted could worldclimb with far more ease if it wasn't for the War of Indenture," Lucky muttered.

And all the more ease for the Perfects to nurck them over as long as it continued, James thought with bitterness.

* * *

"Fast" was relative. When the caravan finally left, the column of machines traveled at slightly faster than a walk---enough to be uncomfortable to walk, as every non-merchant did, but not fast enough to really make much time. James assumed the point was the carrying capacity: even with what seemed to be enough food and other supplies to last for weeks or even months, the machines were only partially full.

They also carried loads of wood to start the fires, four large bonfires to each side. There was not remotely enough for each of the twenty or so parties with them to share the fires, so the lower-rank ones, like James's, stayed to the side.

The fires were neither from incense logs, nor did they have Lucky's sanctuary spells. "Should we---" James started.

Lucky shook her head. "Wouldn't be effective in a group this size, and we can't let others know."

"I've been wondering, Lucky," Ralph said. "Why we shouldn't try to teach the Free Cities how to use---"

At that moment an alarm blared.
