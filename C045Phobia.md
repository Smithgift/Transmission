## Phobia

James grabbed his spear and it shone to life. The spider-rider danced in and out of range, no one quite hitting it. Lucky threw darts, but missed. James threw his bola. It connected with a leg. The spider sagged, and the gremlin fell. With a scream of rage James got the kill, while Ralph kept hacking at the spider until it vanished in red.

Stella sobbed uncontrollably.

"Nurck," Ralph said. "They're going to send a spider-rider every night, aren't they?"

"Yeah," Lucky said. "Or just spiders, period."

"Couldn't you have cast 'sanctuary?'" James demanded.

Lucky cowered. "It wouldn't have mattered! With our _hamartia,_ it would have stopped a small spider but not a spider-rider,"

"James, leave Lucky alone," Stella said, still trembling. "There's nothing we can do about this. I just have to become stronger."

"I'm... nurck, Lucky, I'm sorry," James said. "I'm just... my nerves are shot."

Lucky looked grim. "I forgive you."

"All of our nerves are shot, " Violet chimed in, exhausted. "Let's try to get what remaining sleep we can. Stella, don't feel guilty about staying up. I know you probably can't."

James knew, as long as Stella was afraid, he couldn't sleep either.

* * *

Stella didn't want to talk. Morning eventually came, and James' head had an awful headache, to say nothing of the rage in his heart towards the Perfects.

"Lucky, can you use one of your local area mapping spells to find us a dungeon?" Violet asked.

"No need," Ralph said. "There's always a bunch in the mineshafts."

"But wouldn't they get picked clean?"

"Too dangerous. Knew a guy who had been a miner at a different outpost who decided to go in one with his buddies." Ralph shook his head. "None of them made it. Second time he worldclimbed, he stayed the nurck away from dungeons."

"Our best option would be to attack them in ten days, when we could learn some red spells and use them all," Lucky said. "But I don't think Stella could handle another week of spider-riders."

"I can't. I'm so sorry." Stella looked down at the ground. James put an arm around her shoulders.

"No need to apologize," Ralph said. "You're one of us now, and we watch out for each other."

"Thanks," Stella said quietly.

"Aren't we going to run into this every night, though?" Violet asked.

"As long as we're here, yeah," Lucky said. "Spider-riders only spawn in deserts and mountains. That's also where all the dungeons spawn."

"Nurck," Ralph said.

"I'm so sorry," Stella said. "I should have told you all I was arachnophobic before we made these plans."

"Stella, it's not your fault," James insisted. "It's those dungpits back there that are torturing you."

She straightened herself. "We need the money and items. Let's go dungeon crawling. I'll... I'll do my best to stand the spider attacks."

"Let's get going, then," Ralph said.

* * *

The mountain was simply too big to build the walls of the mining outpost around, but they went through town, anyway. Coming into the mineshafts from off the roads would look shady as nurck. Camping outside might already pose uncomfortable questions. But with the crowds of miners and adventurers coming in and out of the town, James didn't know if anyone would notice.

"James," Stella said. "I think I saw Paul."

James whirled to look, but in the crowd, no one.

"The mechanic? Why him?" Ralph asked.

"He's a stalker," James said.

"James!" Stella interrupted. "We don't know that."

"Might have been a coincidence," Lucky said. "But let's keep an eye out."

No one spotted him again. By the time they reached the large dark mouth to the mineshaft, James had half-decided that it had just been a fluke. Stella was right. Maybe he was just reading things into random coincidences.

The tunnels were lit with a mixture of glowing lamps and patches of that blue bioluminescent moss. Marks on the tunnels, the same style as the Underneath, indicated the location of ore patches and dungeon spawn areas. James had expected minecarts and was not completely disappointed. But they were pulled by luckless miners, protected by armed Transmitted. Perhaps the depths were too dangerous to have automated machinery.

"No, it's just that you can't fit powered wheels on machines that small," Stella said when James asked. "I looked into a more advanced rails system based on pulleys but there's simply no way to fit them in. The boss said it'd been tried and rejected already."

"What's with the guards, then?" James asked.

"Monsters do spawn," Ralph said. "And they're---"

"Contact!" Lucky shouted. Guards of a nearby cart looked up. One threw a dart and struck a shadowy form. Then they moved on.

"At least we're not alo---" James started, but Stella shrieked.

The pile of monster-dropped items included spider legs.

"They're never going to stop, are they?" Violet asked.

"Never," Lucky said. "I don't know if it even matters where we are."

"Then keep moving," Stella said through gritted teeth. "I'll manage. We have to do this."

* * *

Ralph was correct: barely a short walk into the mineshaft they found a large door with glowing Oru symbols. Beside it was a sign reading IT HAS BEEN 34 DAYS SINCE THE LAST IDIOT TRIED THIS DUNGEON. 

"One's good as any other," Ralph mused. "Lucky, what does the Oru say?"

Lucky scanned the door up and down. She didn't answer. James waited.

"Lucky?" Violet asked.

"I..." she paused. "I don't know."
