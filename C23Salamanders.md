## Salamanders

James now understood why the rest of the party was so protective of Lucky. While it wasn't literally every jar, somehow Lucky almost always got great loot. For some reason she kept getting 'those nurcking useless white bowls' as Ralph called them, but even despite that, she found the most crazy things.

And she indeed shied away from touch. When they visited Holds, they always made sure to keep Lucky away from danger, even if it meant squeezing five people in a room meant for two.

But Ralph was right. Their party really was welcome wherever they went, since they had so much loot they could more or less dump it on a Hold to get whatever they needed. More than once James saw a Hold subtly suggest they stick around.

For Lucky's part, she seemed to trust the rest of the party a little, and as far as James could tell she was with them entirely willingly. But she still never got too physically close to anyone, and would sometimes head off by herself.

* * *

One meal around a fire, James looked at his stats, wondering how even if he was probably more elite than Drake's old party now, he still wouldn't qualify as a Salamander among the late Tribe for having too low Trickery.

> Strength A: 16+9\
> Strength B: 16+0\
> Magic: 1\
> Speed: 8+12\
> Trickery: 5\
>
> Magic use: 0%\
> Wounds: 0%

He didn't know how he had managed to gain one more point of Trickery, despite never opening a door or a jar. But somehow he did, and he wasn't going to complain.

"James, are you listening?" Violet asked.

James shook his head. "Sorry. What?"

"We were asking if you felt up to an assault on a Labyrinth," Lucky said.

James would have immediately said yes, earlier. But he knew that a bad assault could lead to him losing his admittedly incredible luck with being in this party. Did he want to risk it all on a roll of the dice?

Yet, he was now skilled with working with them, and even if he didn't have quite as much experience as the others, he still was pretty good at adventuring now.

"Yes," James said.

"Lucky's working out the schedule," Ralph said. 

"If we head to the Labyrinth near the Solar---" Fred started.

"Oh, *no,"* Violet said. "Don't even think about them."

"I thought that's closer to us," James said.

"No, it's too close," Lucky said.

"The Solar Festival Hold is... 'a cult' would be putting it mildly," Ralph said. "The only reason they haven't started a war with nearby Holds is that they'd probably lose, since they've already offended everyone else. Lucky's had a bad experience with them in the past, too."

"The gates are still too close," Lucky said. "We'd arrive on an unlucky day."

"That settles it then," Ralph said. No one but Fred ever argued with Lucky's strange opinions on luck. "What else?"

"If we circle back to near Blue Sunrise, we can hit the one nearby," Lucky said. "We need to wait a day, but then it'll be a perfect day."

"That's..." James started.

They looked at him.

"That's where we---the Dragon Trog Tribe---were destroyed," James said.

"But if the schedule only works that way..." Violet said.

"Lucky, are there any... you know, _other_ lucky days?" Fred asked.

Lucky shook her head. "Not any time soon." She turned to James. "It's going to be OK, James. We won't be attacking in a large group."

"But... I'm not doubting you, Lucky," James started, though on some level he did. "If Labyrinths really are scaled to the number of people going in, why don't Trogs hold back some members?"

"Lucky and I have talked about it," Violet said. "The problem is, no one wants to be left behind, but a worldclimbing Tribe has to worldclimb, right? So it's a situation where they're always going to be throwing more people at the Labyrinth, even if it hurts. And they don't necessarily believe it will."

"Anyway," Ralph said. "So we hit the Labyrinth near Blue Sunrise on the day Lucky really likes. We already have a key. All in favor?"

All but James raised their hands, and James raised his after a moment.

"Then it's decided."

* * *

James couldn't feel a mixture of building worry and excitement as they approached the same gate. At least they wouldn't be stopping by Blue Sunrise, since James didn't know, Lucky or not, _he'd_ be welcome.

They arrived at the Labyrinth gates to find a massive Trog Tribe already camped outside. "Let's keep our distance," Ralph said.

Scouts approached, in the colorful yellow bandannas of the Lion Trogdolyte Tribe. "Hey, what are you doing here?" one asked.

"We're here to assault the Labyrinth," Ralph said. "Don't worry, we're waiting until tomorrow."

"Oh! Funny you should mention that," the scout said. "We'll attack tomorrow, too. We can join forces."

Ralph looked to Lucky, who shook her head vociferously. Fred opened his mouth to speak, but Lucky shot him a death glare, and he didn't. "We'll talk about it," Ralph said, and motioned a thumbs-up towards Lucky.

"All right," the scout said. "You sure you don't want to share our camp at least?"

Ralph looked at Lucky, who shook her head again. "That's OK," Ralph said.

The scout shrugged, and headed back.

"Let's keep a safe distance," Ralph said.

* * *

"Now what?" James asked at the camp that night.

"Lucky, you said the next lucky day was going to be a while, right?" Ralph said.

"A while, yeah," Lucky said. "Tomorrow is our best bet, but not with them."

"I just don't get it," Fred said. "What's special about tomorrow?"

"It's a very lucky day," Lucky said matter-of-factly.

"But _why?_"

"It just is," Lucky insisted.

"We all agreed not to question Lucky's hunches," Ralph said.

"Fine. But at the very least we should join their attack," Fred said.

"Haven't you heard _anything_ of what we've talked about?" Violet said. "The fight is scaled to the number of attackers."

"Fine, fine," Fred said. "I give in. Tomorrow."

"Let's be really cautious, though," Ralph said. "I have to admit I don't trust the Lions. Lucky... would you mind not wandering off tonight?"

"Happy to," Lucky said. "I'll be on watch."

* * *

That night, James was on watch, as was Lucky. He noticed her trembling. He sat a little close to her, but enough to give her plenty of personal space. "Everything OK?" he whispered.

Lucky turned to him, watching him with that strange fear and distance she always showed the rest of the party. "So many things could go wrong tomorrow," she admitted.

"Wasn't tomorrow supposed to be really lucky?" James said in what he hoped was a teasing voice.

"Some things are luck. Some things are up to us. It's the second part I'm worried about."

James didn't know if there was anything he could say to reassure her. "Do you want a hug?" he eventually asked.

She shook her head. "I'm fine."

But later that night, James could hear her soft crying.
