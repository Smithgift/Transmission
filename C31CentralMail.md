## The Town

They did not encounter another patrol, Slaver or Free. By noon the next morning they had found freshly cut stumps, and following them inwards they saw first rich fields of colorful plants then the walls of a town. They got strange looks from farmers as they passed by, but no one tried to stop them.

The guards at the town gate were less sympathetic. The gate itself was small, only enough for maybe one cart, and the walls were made of one continual piece of stone. "More raids these days," one said, hair invisible under his softly shining blue helmet. "How can we tell you aren't spies?"

"We killed a party of Slavers," James suggested.

"Really?" the guard grunted.

Lucky handed him the map. The guard looked at them with slightly less suspicion, but not total acceptance. "How do I know this wasn't your map?"

"If we were Slavers, would we travel in damaged Underneath gear?" Violet asked.

"Maybe, but where's the rest of your Tribe?"

"We were Salamander Wayfarers," Violet said.

"Yes, and with an Unwanted?" the guard asked.

"Since when do Slavers travel with Unwanted?" the other guard, a woman pleaded.

"I don't know. Is that really an Unwanted color under that bonnet?"

Lucky froze.

"Listen," James said firmly. "We have been through a _lot_ of dung over the last few days. We lost one of our members shortly after coming out of the Underneath, we had to ambush Slavers before they ambushed us, and Lucky here has had some trauma about Slavers..."

The male guard paused, objections visibly approaching his lips. "Michael, for nurck's sake you are the most paranoid guard in the entire town," the female guard said.

"Seen enough battles to _be_ paranoid."

"Seven Fords? Greenwald division?" Ralph suddenly asked.

"How did you---" And then suddenly he stared. "_Ralph?_ Really? You worldclimbed again?"

"Wasn't easy, but I can vouch for everyone here."

"Head inside, then, but get some ID, first thing." The guard motioned them in.

Inside, the buildings seemed a mixture of temporary, permanent, and temporary that had become permanent, wood mixed with more of the strange seamless stone. The streets were also of the seamless stuff, but plenty of grass grew everywhere, as well as many plants. "That was close," Violet said.

"Didn't recognize him at first, but I remember the Greenwald Division," Ralph said in a quiet voice. "No idea how many survived, or even if we won."

Lucky came up to James. "I owe you one," she whispered.

James nodded without a word.

* * *

It seemed whenever humans got together in any kind of permanent settlement, on Earth or in the Tower of Worlds, some jerk would eventually reinvent paperwork. They spent the rest of the day filling out forms to at least give some basis to their claims of being ordinary worldclimbers---just ones that had made it as mere Wayfarers.

But the clerk said it was mostly a formality, and once they got their cards, they'd be welcome in most of the Free alliances. The guard Ralph knew could fact-check their identities, and after that...

They ended up renting a room at a tavern in the town. Or attempting to, but before Lucky could even offer a rare stone the smiling proprietor said the first night for worldclimbers was on the house, and so was their first round of drinks. The drinks weren't alcoholic---no one knew how to distill here. But it was the thought that counted.

They kind of sort of celebrated. Maybe it was enough to celebrate just making it to the Surface partially intact. But, James realized, he now had a choice.

Did he try to keep climbing? Or stay here and wait for Stella?

"Nurck, I don't know either," Ralph said when James explained his dilemma. "The Surface is really big, but only parts of it are inhabited, mostly around the Towers."

"The what?" James asked.

"This world's version of Labyrinths," Violet explained. "But getting in them is harder for a variety of reasons."

"Well, we'll probably stick around them, ourselves," Ralph said. "We already worldclimbed once, we can do it again. And you're welcome to climb with us."

James almost said yes. But he knew he had a choice to make. "I promised Stella we'd meet in the second world, though."

The barmaid came up to them. "You're looking for someone?" she asked James.

"Yes. Why?"

"Northwood City---it's a bit south of here, has a system for reuniting people. Lots of married couples getting Transmitted, and looking for each other---"

"We have to go there!" James told his party.

"Sure, why not?" Ralph said. "You've been good to us."

"You do realize there's not a huge chance your girlfriend is up here," Violet said. "And if she's not, you'll be waiting a while."

"I have to take it," James said, determination entering his voice. "This is all because of what happened to her. I can't leave her now."

"I said I'd go. Right, everyone?" Ralph said.

"Right," Violet said.

Lucky didn't seem super crazed about it. But she said "I'll go."

"Then it's settled!" Ralph said, pounding the table with strength enough to rattle the dishes.

* * *

That night, crowded in their room, James heard Lucky cry again. But when he looked at her mouth, he saw it was moving without words.
