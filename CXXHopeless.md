## Hopeless

It was sometime around lunch the next day that they decided they really needed to eat. James walked with his arm around Stella as they looked for whatever kind of mess they had.

More than pleasure, they had shared every bit of themselves with each other. And James knew, no matter what happened, no matter even if he was sent back to the Underneath and killed over and over again, he would _never_ stop trying to find Stella again.

Stella, too, leaned into him more. "Told you things would change. We just needed to wait."

"It was well worth it," James said, and realized it was true.

At the mess hall, James found Lucky seated with the other Perfects at a central table, but the lead Perfect waved them over. Lucky gave the two a knowing look, but said "We have a lot to talk about."

"Yeah?" James asked.

But it was the lead Perfect who spoke. "We didn't introduce ourselves. I'm Odin C, the leader of the Seventh Expedition," he said, offering a hand.. He didn't seem any changed from the previous day, or at least he hid the crushing of his hopes well. "Athena has been telling us of your adventures."

"James Algol," James said, and shook his hand.

"Stella Algol," Stella said, shaking his hand in turn.

"You see..." he paused. "We're stuck."

"You can't worldclimb?" James asked.

"No, and that is why we... were hoping for reinforcements." At last James could see the dashed hopes had had a deeper effect.

"How _do_ you worldclimb from here?" Stella asked.

"An excellent question. There are summoning circles to summon a Guardian, but they require a lengthy amount of continous energy input. We believe we could kill a Guardian, but the problem of powering the circle is more difficult."

"Is it the resources?" James asked.

Odin C shook his head. "We have plenty of those. The problem is that setting up the machines takes time, and there are only so many summoning circles available on this island. Every time we've tried, we've been attacked either at our home base or at our forward outpost. We simply do not have the manpower to defend both at once. One time..." he paused again, with old grief. "We tried bringing everyone to the circle. But a double attack by the other two factions meant that we could not activate the circle, and by the time we got back to our fortress it had been practically razed."

"Tell them about the arrows," Lucky suggested.

"Yes. It seems whomever is in control of the arrows is attempting to keep the peace. They send arrows to each of the factions, but do not help if there is ever conflict between them, and will punish with _hamartia_-growth anyone who tries to start a war."

"It's not you?" James asked, then realized it was a stupid question.

"We just don't know," Lucky said. James realized she had said 'we.'

"Why are the factions angry at each other?" Stella asked. "I can understand the Transmitted might have a grudge against you, but..."

"There used to be just two factions," Odin C said. "Us and Them. But there appears to have been a violent split between them, and the two remaining factions are by no means allied. They simply work together against us."

"And no one's managed to bridge the gap?" Stella asked. "We _should_ all be in this together."

"That's the problem," Lucky said. "The Keepers want to keep everyone here, because if too many people worldclimb then the economy of the Ruins collapses and the survivors die. The Climbers want to keep climbing, but they want to leave a large enough base of Transmitted here so that people can _still_ climb. And then there's a bunch of nomadic bands that hate everyone."

"But isn't the irony that all this fighting means that _no one_ wins?" Stella asked.

"You are correct," the younger Perfect man said. "Thor C," he introduced himself.

"So we're stuck for the moment," Lucky said. "The three of us aren't enough to turn the tide. We just can survive out here for longer."

"Yes, we would love for you to stay," Odin C said. "You can have the best of our resources. Just... don't leave."

James felt uncertain.

"We need to talk about this in private," Stella said.

"By all means do so. But first, enjoy lunch."

* * *

They met in Lucky's new room, which was as fancy as a band of survivors on the Ruins could get, James mused.

"I don't know if the situation is actually as bad as they say," Lucky said. "I think decades of futile struggle have made them allergic to trying again. I know I broke a few times down in the Underneath. But giving up would have been worse."

"Do we know how big the other factions are?" James asked.

"James, we shouldn't fight them," Sella said.

"Each is about twice our size," Lucky said. "And no, I don't want to provoke a war with them, either."

"James, Stella," Lucky pleaded. "We've been struggling for way too long. We all need to rest. You two need to have your honeymoon. I want to be Athena Perl for a while and not Lucky the Unwanted. We can always talk about leaving this later."

James thought about objecting, but it was true. He and Stella could use plenty of _quality_ time with each other. And it didn't sound as if anything bad would happen while they stayed here.

"James, what if we... just stopped?" Stella asked. "Stayed here?"

_"Here?"_ James asked.

"Yes, it's unpleasant and a little dangerous. But right now, all I care about is being with you. And we can be with each other just as easily here as anywhere else."

That was true. "But... what if we never continue?" James asked.

"Why do we need to?" Lucky---Athena asked.

"James, we _won't_ stay here forever," Stella said. "Something will eventually happen. More Transmitted will arrive. Some will die. Eventually the balance of power will shift, or new leaders will arise, or maybe something happens to the Supreme Council on Earth and they send a new expedition after all."

Everything Stella said made complete sense. In fact, why _should_ he argue? He was used to struggling to keep climbing, but why not take a break? At the very least, finish his honeymoon with Stella. He wanted to make up for lost time.

"OK," James said. "We'll stay."

* * *

But that night, wrapped in Stella's arms, he couldn't but wonder who had sent the arrows. What did they want? And who were they, really?
