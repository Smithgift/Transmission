## Expedition

The other Trogs were not particularly torn up about Amanda's 'death.' "People get popped," Drake told him as the camp fortified itself for fourth watch. "Happens all the time. If you get brokenhearted every time you'll eventually run out of heart."

"Drake," Kate said, shaking her head, then turning to James. "It's really hard the first few times, but you get used to it. She's not dead. She's just somewhere out there."

"Being murdered over and over again by monsters," James said, his voice hollowed.

"She has more survival skills now," Drake said. "And she'll be found by another Trog Tribe eventually, or maybe even spawn inside a Hold."

James had also learned that 'loyalty' was a flexible concept to Trogs. Since it was not uncommon that an entire Tribe would be destroyed, especially when fighting a Guardian, there was no real concept of an oath of fealty or tribal rituals. Everything felt uncomfortably transient.

James hadn't realized how, as bad Earth had been, that the little good Perfects did---little as it was---had actually been good. A Worker wasn't guaranteed a lifetime position, but if you had the certifications chances were you'd end up at the same kind of job over and over again, usually under the same Thinkers. Here, you weren't even guaranteed to make it to the next day.

The causalties had been small, though: only three lost before the troll band attack had been repulsed. And James had proven himself enough in combat to join a party---though not a high-level one like Drake's.

It would still be some days of marching before they reached their new destination. But thankfully, they'd stay there for a while.

"What happened to your trees?" Gus asked.

James tapped his nose with his thumb. His white tree was slightly larger, but of all things, his black tree had shrunk slightly. "I have no idea."

"No one does," another partymember muttered.

* * *

The smaller tunnels of the deeper caves in the Underneath made his heart jitter. He had not merely one weapon, but two, and was with a five-person party, three of whom were experienced warriors with powerful gear. This was standard protocol among Trogs to bloody new Trogs in real combat, while reducing the risk. So he knew he was about as safe as he could be in this world, and he had to get stronger to get to the second world, but he still worried.

"Relax," Peter, their leader, a Worker, said to him. "This is life in the Underneath, unless you want to lay around in a Hold."

"Yes, sir," James said.

One partymate made notches on the cave walls as they traveled. James had been briefed on the marks, but he didn't know them well enough to read them at sight. James's job was to spot anything that could be useful, such as fruit or certain rare rocks. If he spotted an ore vein, even better, and they'd go back immediately to the camp for a mining expedition to form. He didn't know what they looked like, but he had been told they were so blindingly obvious that he'd know immediately.

But no ore veins. They traveled deeper and deeper, strangely without monsters. Then at a seemingly arbitrary depth, they stopped.

"This is where you start finding rooms and there's a good chance of finding newts," Peter said. "Lower, and you risk more dangerous mons---Contact!"

They whirled into a circular formation, spears out, looking for the threat. A lone snake came their way, and one of the experienced Trogs took out a strangely shaped dart and nailed it. Another experienced Trog reached out with his spear and hooked the loot back, still staying in the circle. "You don't want to get in close combat with those things," Peter said. "There's a small but significant chance their poison will kill you instantly."

"It doesn't drain your HP?" James asked, then realized it was a stupid question. "I mean---grow your wounds?"

"I knew what you meant," Peter said. "Nope. Either you get off fine or you instantly die. But it can still do a lot of damage even if the poison doesn't kill you instantly. Quiet!"

They stood still. James heard the skittering of far too many legs. From a corner burst a swarm of hideous spiders with slightly misshapen body parts. He hesitated for one moment then thrust out his spear and bagged one. Other mutant spiders sprayed acid, but the formation countercharged and slaughtered them.

"Yeah, spiders are the worst," Peter said. "Their acid does some wear to your equipment, and your only option is to charge them before they get you. Reverse of the snakes, in a way. Quiet."

They all fell silent again.

"No, nothing. Let's go."

They walked on in the caves. "Are there normally so many monsters?" James asked.

"It seems they either spawn in swarms, or you barely see any," a different Trog suggested. "Never just the same amount."

"And no one has any idea how or why," Peter mused.

"Maybe it's statistics?" a Thinker girl, a somewhat more experienced newt, asked. "Sometimes there's just randomly more or less?"

"Yeah, but---contact!" 

This time the troll was on the other side of the party, and Peter got it with his spear.

"I was about it to say it's always at the most inconvenient times," Peter finished. "Let's keep moving. It's not worth it to stand around and grind monsters---the loot isn't that good, and it doesn't train your stats enough."

They moved on. James felt himself, though still on edge, relax just a little. They were well-equipped for the creatures that had tormented him earlier. And even if they were wounded, they had some salves that would lower the wound-meter temporarily---not that they'd needed to use even one.

* * *

But as if in comfirmation, there wasn't another monster attack before they encountered the stone door covering the entrance to a tunnel. Strange symbols glowed on it, again with no seeming interpretation. Why 'interpretation?' James wondered at his own thought. It felt too detailed to be generic runes that a game designer just slapped on for decoration.

"Every room has a door trap, a room curse, or both," Peter told them. "And there's inevitably a monster inside. Opening the door _might_ not trigger a room curse, but trying to break into it through the walls always does. And trying the door _might_ set off the door trap, but it's still better a door trap than a room curse."

"What's a room curse?" James asked.

"Some kind of nasty thing that happens if you break some kind of unknown rule," Peter said. "There's pools for both. Now, James and Fred, you're on the left, Paula and me, you're on the right. Raoul's going to try the door."

They formed the formation, a kind of V, with the ends of the legs facing outward. The point, as far as James could tell, was to protect against attacks from either direction. He wanted to turn his head and see, but he was better as a lookout than the front line.

"Nork," Raoul muttered. "No trap."

"OK. Everyone face the door. Be prepared for something to go very wrong when we step inside. But we're going to try and rush the monster instead before that wrong thing happens. Ready? Three, two, one, GO!"

They charged inside to find a ginormous scorpion with an unpleasant number of claws waiting for them. James roared a battle cry and charged, but only screamed as he found himself slammed against the ceiling by invisible forces. The Trogs killed the scorpion in seconds and James came tumbling down. The ground hurt on impact.

"Levitation on the newt," Peter mused. "Least bad room curse."

"Yeah, and you're not the one who got levitated," James said, brushing himself off. He checked his stats to see his wound meter about twenty-five percent full.

"Could have been much worse if one of us three got cursed and couldn't help," an experienced Trog noted, which James had to admit inwardly was true.

"By the way, chances were you hit the wrong pitch," Peter added.

"...What?"

"One of the room curse triggers is hitting the wrong pitch. Or volume, even. We're not sure which."

_"What?"_

"I know."

Meanwhile the Trogs had gathered around the far side of the room, where five circular jars with lids stood.

"We each get one jar," Peter said to James.

"Do we need to check the jars for traps?" James asked.

"The jars are never trapped," Peter said. "Only the rooms and doors are. Figure that one out."

One by one the Trogs opened a jar, inventory screens popping up. James with some eagerness opened his.

Inside was a sword, what appeared to be a bola, and a colorful piece of paper.

"Oh, sweet," Peter told him. "That's a scroll."

"A scroll?" James asked, examining it.

"Yeah, you can use it cast a magic spell. Only once, though. You'll get somewhere around twenty credits for it."

James stared. That was one fifth of his debt paid for.

"Let's head back," Raoul suggested. "If this is a bad monster day, I don't want to be too far from camp."

"Agreed," Peter said. "Off we go."

* * *

When they were back, James felt relief of tension he didn't even realize he had. Even in the depths of the Underneath, he was still pretty safe as long as he was with experienced Trogs. Plus, he had gotten some sweet loot.

Or so he had hoped.

Boris looked over his inventory. "A scroll on your first trip? You must be one of the lucky ones."

"How much?" James asked eagerly.

"It's _reduce hunger,_ so ten credits." 

He felt stung. "Only _ten?_" 

"It's a common scroll, and not very useful. It has the same effect of eating a full meal. But it's popular among elite Wanderers, since you can carry a lot more scrolls than meals. So ten credits."

James was still disappointed, but he had to admit it wasn't like he could use the scroll, either. "Can I keep the other stuff?"

"If you want, but you probably want to pay off your gear first. But tell you what: keep the sword, and give me back the loaned short sword. The bola could go either way, but it's hard to use well, so you're probably better getting credits for it and learning your present weapons better."

"Agreed," James said.

* * *

He stepped out into the camp proper and began wandering out. He found himself, of all things, happy. He had found a community, they accepted him, and even if he kept asking noob questions and making noob mistakes, eventually he'd be an elite Trog like the rest.

But, he mused, that was never the point. The point was making it to the next world. Then he had to find Stella, somehow.

Strange, though. Why did these systems work like this? And what even was this place? Did the Perfects really spend all these resources in creating a place that made no sense, where even it seemed the laws of physics were different, instead of Rebuilding the Earth as they were supposed to? But if they created this place, why not create a world where they themselves were immortal?

None of it made sense. Stella might be able to figure it out, as she was a Thinker truly worthy of the name. But he had to find her first, or her, him.
