## Stats

They set off the next day, after getting their ID cards. Without the ability to take pictures, the cards just had their descriptions, but it would still likely be enough, James mused. As long as no one wanted to see under Lucky's bonnet.

The road to Northwood City was not long, and thankfully it was still heavily forested. Due to the need to keep the fires burning, most cities were near natural or artificial forests. James still noticed that the road they travelled had large patches of stumps along both sides---travelers wanting safety, or at least what they thought would bring them safety.

To find one of Lucky's special trees, they had to go significantly off the path---but no one wanted a nasty surprise overnight.

"Hey, Lucky," James asked that night. "How do you shrink your trees?"

Lucky was staring into the flames, as if looking at things only she could see. "You don't," she said after a pause. "They both just shrink over time."

"So, wait," James said. "We're just stuck with high _hamartia_ until it cools down?"

"I don't make the rules here," Lucky snapped.

"Everyone, let's calm down," Ralph said, and the tension dissipated ever so slightly. "We'll just have to live with the facts as they are."

"Yeah," Lucky mumbled.

"Do you know if we're still over the threshold?" Violet asked.

"Let's look at our trees," Lucky said.

Even if James knew the karma or whatever-the-nurck the creators of this place called it decayed over time, he could have sworn his trees hadn't shrunk at all, not even a twig.

"We're still over the spawning threshhold," Lucky said. "I do have a spell that increases the threshold, but I have a limited number of blue scrolls to power it. The right stars aren't shining."

"When the stars were shining, why didn't you use it?" James asked.

"I did. I just didn't tell any of you." She looked around at them, and James could see the twitch of fear in her.

"Let's burn a scroll," Ralph suggested. "We can always buy more."

Lucky obediently took out a scroll---James couldn't tell it from any other---and tossed it into the fire. It blazed blue for a moment. "_Oru Aru Ali Paru Paru Pari,_" she chanted. Nothing seemed to change, but she added. "We should be safe, now."

James wondered what language she spoke, since it didn't seem like any he'd ever heard. But the Perfects did censor a lot about the past. "Night, all," he said, and lay down on his sleeping roll.

* * *

No monsters attacked during the night. The next morning, they went on their way, and by the trail markers, they'd reach Northwood City the same day.

James walked up close to Lucky. She looked up at him---James realized that after all he had said about Perfects, she might never fully trust him. If she ever fully trusted anyone anymore. "Hey, you said the magic stat is called Crane?"

"Yeah. Let me teach you all the paragon-animals, so you know what the stats do," she offered, as if a peace offering.

They gathered around her.

"From the top, the first stat is Lion, mislabeled Strength A, the stat trained by courage and charging into battle. It increases your ability to attack.

"The second stat is Gorilla, mislabeled Strength B, trained by enduring heavy exercise or injuries. It helps you _do_ heavy exercise or withstand injuries.

"The third stat, mislabeled magic, is Crane, trained by doing magic, which both increases the power of magic and the amount you can do.

"The fourth stat, mislabeled Speed, is an animal we're not sure about, but we believe it is some kind of fast-running hare or bird. We call it Runner It's increased by walking, especially running, and makes you move faster.

"And the fifth stat, mislabeled Trickery, is another animal we're unsure about, possibly a spider or snake. Whatever it was, it was an ambush predator, so we call it Ambush. It's trained by either surviving or initiating ambushes, and helps you with either of those things."

"Wait, wait, 'Trickery' is _Ambush?_" Violet asked. "How on Earth does it get trained? How can the system tell?"

"The system is a lot smarter than we give it credit for."

Meanwhile, James looked at his stats with new knowledge.

> Lion: 24+5\
> Gorilla: 32+1\
> Crane: 1\
> Speed: 16+6\
> Ambush: 8+1\
>
> Magic use: 0%\
> Wounds: 0%

"Huh, so that time we ambushed the slavers really did increase our Ambush," James asked.

"But how does it _work?_" Violet pleaded. "It's not like the system could help us trick them, could it?"

Lucky shrugged. "We don't know if the system can affect our nervous systems. The problem is, how could we test it? To have a real ambush, the people you're ambushing can't be letting themselves be ambushed as a test."

"You _tested_ it?"

"We tried. Why?"

"So you didn't just learn from the manual?"

Lucky shrugged again. "I don't know, and I don't want to talk about this any more."

"But---"

"James, enough," Ralph said.

They walked on in awkward silence. James realized he didn't know why Lucky was suddenly sharing all this with them. Or why she suddenly wanted to stop. He'd assumed it was because she was afraid they'd turn on her if she didn't... but why? She had to know by now they wouldn't try to hurt her.

But maybe the wounds of people who had were deep enough that she never truly trust them.

James decided he would have to back off a little. Let Lucky talk when she was ready. Not now, apparently. He had just been curious.

But why was the question of a manual so sensitive?
