## Vengeance

"Serves him right," James said as the distant screaming continued.

"It doesn't!" Stella said. "We should help him."

"We can't," Ralph said. "We don't have torches, and we don't even really know where he---"

James heard the twang of a bow. "Contact!" Lucky shouted.

He drew his sword, thought better of it, opened his magic menu, and shot a flame spear at the spider-rider. The fire incinerated them both.

Paul was screaming in the distance, and then... silence.

Stella cried again, and James wrapped his arms around her. "At least---at least I'm not woken up by them," she said with shaking breaths.

"Yeah," Lucky said. "Vulkan knows my benefactors will just send a non-spider monster. So I bet he's sending spider-riders as soon as possible."

Meanwhile, James listened. Nothing, no begging, no cries of battle. Just silence.

"D-D-O we go investigate?" Stella asked, breath quivering.

"Whatever happened, it's all over now," Ralph said, shaking his head. "Let's get a good night's sleep."

* * *

Paul did not come to bother them in the night. Nor even in the next morning---no sign of him anywhere. "Let's not look," James suggested, and though Stella and even Lucky looked guilty, they went their way.

But James did think. Paul may have been a dungpit, but he was not the first who knew Lucky had the means to get magic, and he wouldn't be the last. If they so much as said the word "Oru" in a city, there was a risk someone could overhear and ask uncomfortable questions. Or, really, if someone even just looked under Lucky's bonnet, as tight as she wore it at all times.

Violet, meanwhile, was wondering something different. "Sending a Titan costs thousands of credits, right?" she asked.

"4,096 credits, to be exact."

"And a credit is worth...?"

"It varies. My mom... she was the one doing the purchasing for our family." Lucky trailed off. "She said once there's some kind of auction. Not sure why. But usually around a hundred Earth credits for one Tower of Worlds credit."

"So Vulkan Lisp spent somewhere around four hundred thousand Earth credits to send a Titan."

"Yes. Or he pooled with friends and/or his sycopants."

"Can he _keep_ doing that?"

Lucky stopped in thought, and the rest stopped to look at her. "Maybe not. Especially if he needs to ramp up the local _hamartia_ enough to get to the threshold."

"So maybe we can stay in cities from now on?"

Lucky shook her head. "It's risky. And now the Perfects know we think that."

"The Perfects can go nurck themselves!" James shouted. "_Every_ time we have a plan, we start asking whether they'll try to sabotage it. Every nurcking time! And guess what, we're like 66% of the way off this world!"

"James, calm down," Stella said. "We shouldn't get overconfident."

"I know, but unless we have some kind of secret code that the Perfects don't understand, they know what we say, like it or not."

Lucky hesitated for a split second. "We can't let our guard down," she said.

"I know, but..." James groaned out loud. "It's been _weeks_ of this."

"Actually, it's been less than two weeks, I think," Stella said. "But I know what you mean. I'm sick of spiders every night."

"We'll be out of the desert soon," Lucky said. "And then we can not go back in."

"That's why I was asking about the Titans," Violet said. "Couldn't we head to a big city? As we've seen, Titan attacks are survivable, and maybe Vulkan Lisp is out of credits. Maybe we could find a job and make the rest of the money we need? Because... otherwise, Stella will have to suffer every night."

"I'd... I'd be willing," Stella said. "I can become stronger."

"No," James said, kissing her on the lips. "You're already very strong. We just need to find a way to make money that doesn't involve spiders." A sentence, James realized, he would never have spoken on Earth.

"I dunno," Ralph said, shaking his head. "We have Lucky's magic ring, so I don't think any dungeon could really stop us. It's just the spider-riders around the mountains and deserts."

"Tell you what," Lucky said. "We'll worry about our next steps once we have the next key fragment refined. No use putting a cart before the horse. Or build the bridge before the river, as the Oru said."

"They must have been very much like us," Stella said.

"Like I said, they were human enough."

They walked on in thoughtful silence.

* * *

They were ready for the spider-rider that night, and when it came Stella fried it with her own fire spear. She still shook afterwards. "I hate this so much."

"We'll figure out something," James said, but knew it would still be days before they got out of the wastelands back to the forest.

* * *

For whatever Vulkan got off on, petty spite seemed high on the list, and he sent a spider-rider every night. But nothing else, and Lucky noted their destiny trees didn't seem to change too much, and even slowly shrank. Was Vulkan plotting something? Or, as James did not quite dare hope, he had run out of money?

James was about as relieved as Stella when they saw the forest's edge one morning.

Then they spotted the road. "Well, paint me in sauce and call me nurcking pasta, because that's the road to Northwood City," Ralph said. "We arrived in the right spot."

"Let's hurry then," Lucky said. "I just want to relax for a bit."

"We all do," Violet said, and they all laughed, even Lucky.

* * *

Word of Ironspire's desperate yet successful defense had reached Northwood long before them, and the guards were arguing about it before the gate. "Every single person insists he saw green giants," the woman guard pleaded. "They were big and bright enough to be seen anywhere in the city."

"Yeah, well, if someone can magically summon green giants, I hope he can help with the war," the other guard muttered. Then looked up. "You from Ironspire?"

"Yes," James said, as the party dug out their IDs.

"Is this dung about green giants real?"

"Sure is," James said. "Did most of the damage to the Eyedra, I think. I'm just glad whoever controlled them was on our side." Lucky gave him a pleading look.

"I told you they're real!" the female guard insisted to the other.

"I guess you're right."

"I _always_ am!"

"So you say." As if belatedly realizing he was on duty, he motioned to the party. "Move along."

* * *

Once inside, Stella insisted on heading immediately to the factory where she used to work. "My boss is a total morning person," she said. "If we're going to pitch something, it should be this early."

"I'll come with with you," James said.

"I won't," Lucky said. "I don't want to mess up their enchanting with my color item. _Especially_ if it's the key fragment we're trying to refine." She opened up her inventory, and with a bit of sorting Stella had everything they needed.

In fact, it turns out that loads of people had beaten them to the punch. The moment James and Stella stepped into the bright noisy factory, Stella's boss spotted her. "Stella!" he said with the gleam of happy upper management in his eyes. "We could use you! We've been flooded by Eyedra materials from Ironspire's defense, and we can barely refine them fast enough."

"Actually," Stella said sheepishly. "We were coming to see if we could pitch you on us using your equipment to refine our own."

The gleam dropped marginally. "I could offer you an employee discount if you were, to... err, consult for a bit. I haven't a clue how to lay out the machines without you, I admit."

"Sure. What we really need is a key fragment..." Stella followed the boss down the hallways, and James followed her into a large room.

James had imagined the Titan heart materials were melted down and cast, or maybe ground into pieces into shape. He wasn't quite wrong about the principle, just not the... mechanics.

An enormous machine like a long-armed juggler spun pieces around with a horrendous racket, spraying colorful fluids in a kind of bizarre 3D-printing into a gem in a central pillar. He couldn't take his eyes off the ludicrous process, finally understanding why Stella had thought refining it would be a problem.

"Yes, I can definitely help," Stella called over the noise. "We can discuss the details, but the big thing is---"

"James Algol and Stella Cobol?" a voice demanded.

James turned to see a patrol of armed guards, weapons at the ready.

"I am Stella Cobol," Stella said calmly, but loud enough to be heard. "Who are you?"

"We're with the Free Alliance Unified Police, and you're under arrest."
