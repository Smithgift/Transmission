## Too many arthropods

James spotted the big spider first. And it wasn't just big, it was nurcking enormous. Some synapse in his brain---or whatever the Oru had stuck in his skull---triggered and he thought to charge inside rather than check on Stella. Then he saw all the other spiders.

Lucky shouted something, but James didn't hear. He needed to kick some spinnerets! He stabbed one spider only for two more to charge him while a spray came from behind. Ralph charged in beside him and cleaved a spider in two. The third spun around and raced towards the women. He threw a bola and bagged it, but then more spiders swarmed him.

A fire spear shot out and struck the big spider. It reared and spat out---more spiders. Nurck!

"Kill the big one!" Lucky shouted.

James ran inside and stabbed his spear into one leg, pulled it out, stabbed into another leg---and they slammed into the ceiling. Another fire spear struck the big spider. 

The levitation ended and he collapsed back down. Spiders crawled atop of him, scratching and spraying acid. He tried to fight them, but one snatched his spear in its mandibles and yanked it away.

Then a roar-scream of defiance came from Stella. The big spider hissed. All the ones on top of James rushed for Stella.

Lucky ripped a scroll and a ray shot out, then became a violent mushroom cloud that fried the big spider instantly, as well as cooking numerous other spiders.

"Careful with the rest!" Ralph shouted. "James is injured!"

Violet came to him and pulled out a salve. James hurt all over, and saw his armor was in tatters. Nurck. He had just gotten it. Lucky came by and drew her Phoenix Sword, standing guard. Stella was hunting down the remaining spiders with screams of rage and fear and Ralph got the rest.

And then, silence.

"James," Lucky said, anger in her voice. "I was going to fry them all when I saw the crowd. But you had to run into them so I couldn't just _firestorm_ them all."

"_Firestorm_? That rare scroll we were going to learn?" Violet asked, not turning from James's wounds.

"Yes, and I burned it here because I thought Stella was going to be out for the count," Lucky said. "No offense!" she called out.

"None... none taken," Stella said, sitting down. "I'm... I'm going to be OK. I just need... quiet. I'll be fine."

James knew she wasn't, but he wasn't getting there as long as Violet was getting the salves on him. "Turn over," she said. He did and realized how much acid he had been exposed to on the other side.

"Why did the Oru put item durability in this stupid game-world?" he muttered out loud. "Don't they know how much that mechanic sucks?"

"Who knows what the Oru thought was 'fun', or if they even cared?" Lucky asked. "We're dealing with a culture that had no shared ludic touchpoints with ours, if they even thought in those terms."

Stella, meanwhile, was crying into Ralph's arms. He needed to be there! He crawled forward, only to be grabbed by Violet. "Hold still! Don't make me waste salve!"

"Fine," James said, and rested. Stella got up and sat on the floor by him.

"I don't think I could do this again," Stella said. "Maybe. If I had to. But I'm... I'm shot. I hate spiders."

"Believe me, I do too, now," James said.

Lucky went to the center of the room, where one giant jar awaited, untouched. James watched her pull out... a scroll.

"That's it?" James called.

"It must be one of the ultra-rare ones," Violet said.

"Probably. I'm not familiar with this one." She stowed it in her inventory. "Let's get going. It might be getting dark outside."

* * *

Once they stepped out, the characters on the dungeon door glowed, then faded, then disappeared altogether. Then the door itself disappeared, the wall becoming purely smooth.

"We did it," James said, hopping up with a whoop. His magic boots made him hop higher and he nearly crashed into the ceiling yet again.

"And we nearly died like five times," Lucky grumbled. "Let's be more careful next time."

"But we did win," Ralph said thoughtfully.

By the time they had gotten out of the mine, through Ironspire, and out some distance, the sun was almost down, and James was totally exhausted. He knew that, unless that dungpit Perfect was asleep, he'd be sending a spider-rider tonight. And Stella knew it, too, which was why she was already at the verge of tears.

"Stella, if you can sleep, try," Lucky said. "But if you can't, you can just be on watch."

"I don't think I can sleep," she said. "Knowing..."

"I can stay up," James ventured.

"If you can, _you_ should sleep," Stella insisted. "We shouldn't all be sleepless."

"Can't sleep if you're in danger, dear," James said.

"We'll be in danger non-stop until we're in the fourth world. Get. Some. Sleep."

"As you command," James said, and started to undo the remains of his armor. He did feel very, very sleepy. But if Stella was in... danger...

* * *

Stella's scream woke him up, and he grabbed his spear to see the spider-rider taking potshots at a distance with its bow. Lucky ripped a scroll and shot a fire spear at it, toasting it.

Stella breathed deeply. "I'm... I'm never going to get used to this. I'm sorry. I'm just so afraid of spiders."

James didn't know how to answer, so he went and gave her a hug.

* * *

The next morning, the party looked through their loot-filled inventories for the good stuff. Most of it was high-end equipment, but they had a number of rare material drops, and of course the strange scroll they got from the boss.

"'Let us recall the ancient heroes,'" Lucky read the opening words. "It seems to be a section from Oru scriptures about famous warriors in their past. But some of the names are Thinkers."

"How can you tell?" James asked.

"They named their castes based on great achievements or technological marvels, it seems. We Perfects did the same thing: all our last names are the bloodlines of the original Oru gene splices, named after pre-Collapse programming languages."

James didn't know why that felt so unsettling, but it did.

"So what does it do?" Stella asked.

"Beats me. Summons something? Gives massive stat boosts? Makes materials better?"

"Speaking of that, can we get enchanting?" Violet asked. "I'm curious how this works."

"Let me check one thing first," Lucky said, scratching symbols into the sand. "You should all learn this, too, I guess. See those little symbols under the words at the top of the scroll?" They all peered over her shoulder. "We don't know what the Oru called them, but we call them rubytext, the phonetic and tonal readings of the characters. One reason we believe the texts are written in archaic Oru, and their scripture, is that there'd be no reason to put this kind of aid in their world if the words were just ordinary speech."

Lucky then wrote numbers in Oru below the copied rubytext. "Now each phoneme and tone has a numerical value, so you multiply them together per character. Then you add them up to get the numerical values of each character and you get... this number." Lucky wrote 2048+448+8+2, the Oru equivalent of 2506, at the end. "And we see it's a green spell. Awesome. We can learn it this tomorrow, when the green stars shine." She stuffed it back in her inventory.

James looked, fascinated. He considered himself good with numbers---you know, good, normal, _real_ numbers. Counting. Fractions. Ratios. That he understood. But Stella once told him there were numbers that didn't work like that, that weren't really fractions, and pi was one of them, and _those_ were the real numbers. It never made any sense to him, but this Oru stuff looked like good ol' multiplication. "Let's learn Oru from you," he suggested.

"Yeah," Violet said. "And then we can chant the spells like you do."

"Sure," Lucky seemed a little embarrassed.

"Let's also work on the enchanting," Stella said, as if uncomfortable.

"James," Ralph suggested. "How about you and I go shopping for better armor? Yours in bad shape."

James really did want to watch the enchanting "Uh, OK," James said.

* * *

Ralph, it turned out, wanted to talk.

"I get she's your fiancée," he said as they wandered Ironspire in search of an armorer. "And I understand you care for her. But you can't break formation to try and save her."

James didn't want to answer that, so he didn't.

"I understand," he said. "The units in the Free army that had married couples had the worst casualties of us all. But it's just not something we can risk going forward."

"Ralph, dude," James snapped. "I wasn't thinking. You know how much Stella is afraid of spiders."

"Then at the very least stand back to keep her safe!" Ralph thundered. "We had a chokepoint with the door! Lucky could have hammered them with fire and someone else with darts. If it wasn't for Stella charging in at the end, we'd all have died."

James had to admit he was right. "Fine," he said. "I messed the nurck up." He decided to change the subject a little. "Where were you in all the chaos?"

"Trying to keep the spiders off Lucky. Biggest nurckload of spiders I'd ever seen."

"Me and you, both."

They came to a store. "Let's see if we can find something your size..."

* * *

They didn't quite, but James was used to armor not quite fitting. Unless a Transmitted was loaded enough to have his own personal armor made, he just had to make do, at least in the Underneath and Surface. What about the higher worlds? "Hey, Ralph," James asked as they approached the gates. "Do you know anything about the third world?"

Ralph shook his head. "Knew a guy who claimed he'd been there, and I know it's called the Heavens, but no more than that."

"You know, how _did_ people know the names of the worlds?" James asked.

"Lucky's never corrected us about the names, so I guess they're the Oru names. Perfects must have figured it out."

"But how did all the rest of the Transmitted find out?"

"Maybe they're---" they stopped when they heard Stella and Lucky's voices echoing in the distance with high-pitched delight. They hurried up to see Lucky practically glowing. No, _literally_ glowing, her whole body shined faintly red.

"What kind of crazy stuff did you make?" James asked eagerly.

"Oh, all our enchantments failed," Stella said absentmindedly.

"That's the bad news," Lucky said, unconcerned. "But this right here is the very, _very_ good news." She held up her finger, on which was a ruby ring shining red.
