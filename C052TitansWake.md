## Titan's Wake

James turned to see Violet had frozen solid, her eyes staring ahead in terror. Stella was trying to pry off the ice with her bare hands, to no avail.

"Stop!" Ralph commanded. "Don't dislodge the sword!"

Yes, as long as Violet still had the Phoenix Sword wrapped in her hand, she couldn't die. And the fact that she hadn't disappeared was proof she was still alive, even if unbreathing.

"Lucky!" James called.

"Wait," she said, her voice containing unsteady calm. "Magic side effects are temporary."

"What do you mean, temp---"

James didn't have to finish his sentence. The ice started cracking, then melted away in an instant. Violet gasped for breath as if she had never once breathed before, then immediately began hyperventilating.

"Violet, please, calm down," Stella said, in a voice little calmer herself. "You don't want to hurt your lungs."

Violet's breathing slowed, but she still shook. "I'll never fill my magic meter again," she said. "That was _awful._"

"Magic meter?" James asked.

"I warned you all not to fill your magic meter," Lucky said, firm, but not scolding. "The potential side effects can be lethal."

"I knew that," Violet said. "I was watching. But I had to use one more fire spear to save James." She took a more calm gulp of air. " I feel like death," she admitted. "Never again."

James opened up his stats screen.

> Lion: 24+7\
> Gorilla: 40+0\
> Crane: 5\
> Runner: 24+1\
> Ambush: 8+7\
>
> <???>
>
> Magic use: 98%\
> Wounds: 24%

"That could have ended up much worse," James admitted. At least his Crane had skyrocketed, either from casting so many spells or being banged around by the Eyedra's spells.

"That's why I've been warning you," Lucky insisted.

Then James spotted the strange character, glowing in dark brown. "What's that weird symbol?"

"Lingering status effect," Lucky said. "Sometimes curses stick around for a cycle or two. It reads 'weight'---looks like things will be heavier for a bit."

Great. Another mechanic Earth game designers had abandoned: having items actually have weights, as opposed to just abstracting it all away for more symbolic forms of inventory capacities. He went to open his inventory and... nothing happened.

"Or that," Lucky mused. "I guess you can't use your inventory for a while."

"Absolutely nurcking great," James said. Then realized, for as lame as _this_ was, Violet had nearly died in an excruciatingly horrible way. "Nothing compared to what you dealt with, Violet."

"I'm not offended," she said, getting up. With some careful movements, she checked her own stats. "I'll survive. You can untie the sword."

Ralph helped her unknot it.

James took the bandana and tied it back around his head. Then it hit him. "Ralph! You got the last hit, right? We'll get the loot!"

"No use running," Ralph said. "Rule for Titans in every Free city I've ever been in is they distribute the parts equally to everyone who fought."

"But Lucky did like half the damage!" James protested.

"I doubt it was half," Lucky said. "But even if it was, I'm not going to volunteer that I have magic. Let's get going, though." With nodding, they walked on towards the corpse of the Eyedra.

* * *

Ironspire had been partially leveled by the battle. Some structures had survived with only scratches from rained debris. Others had been reduced to complete rubble. James saw piles of equipment where unfortunate Transmitted had met their demise. Fires still burned in some pits, while other pits lay in suspiciously eyeball-shaped craters. More than once, they had to change streets because the detritus had rendered it impassible.

"I have to admit," Ralph said. "I never met anyone who got a last hit on a Titan. No one is ever going to believe me."

"The key fragment Lucky already has was from the Great Wyrm," Stella said. "If we get enough heart material to refine one more gem..."

They were more than halfway there. "Now that Lucky has that super-OP summoning spell---" James started.

"I don't. Sort of," Lucky interrupted.

He turned to see her frowning. "What?" James asked. "But you cast it several times!"

"That wasn't a red spell. That was that scroll we got from the spider boss, which is green. But when that cycloptic worm came near, it cast a green spell on you. Then I realized, duh, it's the next day. The green stars are shining tonight. So I could learn _and_ cast it."

"So on the days the stars are right, you can cast it again repeatedly?" Stella asked.

"Yes. But that's two days out of every sixty-four."

"It's still pretty OP," James said.

"Yeah. Probably why it was that level of reward."

"And speaking of rewards..." Violet said as they approached the remains of the walls. A line of Transmitted had formed, joking with nervous exhaustion or high-fiving each other. At the end a guard shared inventories with each person.

The party got in at the end. James still felt they deserved more than usual, since Lucky had done so much. At the same time, he knew the cannons had to have done a large chunk of the damage, and chances were some of the cannoneers hadn't made it. And even the ordinary Transmitted who had just held the line against the waves of worms the Eyedra had sent out deserved _something._

But really, it didn't matter as long as they got sufficient material.

Their turn came quickly. James wondered if they had arrived late, and then to a deep, sinking horror realized that the fifty or so Transmitted in line or celebrating with their party to the side were the only survivors. Unless some had managed to flee, the Eyedra had killed the vast majority of Ironspire's population.

He came up to the front. "Name?" the guard asked.

"James Algol," he said.

Another guard wrote it down, and then the first guard stretched out his inventory.

"Erm, I can't access my inventory."

"What?" the guard asked.

"It's a status effect." He showed the guard his stats screen. "See where it says... err. That symbol. I think it means I can't access---I mean, I know it means---"

"Just open your inventory," the guard said.

James tapped his nose. "See? I can't open it."

The guard muttered a curse to himself. "Well, here, I'll just hand it to you." He dug out one gently glowing brown rock, one shattered fragment of a lens, and one large dead worm. "Good work tonight. Next!"

Each of them got the same loot, as far as James could tell, although the guard seemed unhappy to be sharing loot with an Unwanted. But he gave Lucky thanks anyway and sent her on the way.

"Now what?" James asked as he gratefully passed his share for Lucky to store.

"We have enough," Lucky said.

James whooped.

"How are we going to refine it?" Stella asked.

James's stomach dropped. "What do you mean, 'refine?'"

"The costly, lossy process to turn raw Titan heart materials into a key fragment," Lucky said. "Which means, once again, we need money."

"We could easily sell the rest of the materials for a large sum," Stella said. "We just need to find a factory that will have the machinery."

"How about that factory you worked at?" James asked.

"You're right!" Stella gleamed. "I'm sure they'll still want to charge us a fee, but I could tell them I'll handle all the engineering. I know what to do, it's just the power to do it."

"We'll head back to Northwood, then," Ralph said. "But right now, I just want to sleep."

"Keep a watch," Lucky said. "But there's no way they can spawn anything tonight."

They passed by a fire still burning in its pit. "Let's stop here," James said.

No one disagreed.

James thought he would stay up and talk to Stella, but the moment he lay down he fell instantly asleep.

* * *

That night, he dreamed of eye monsters and being frozen alive. And it wasn't even a sensible nightmare, it just kept repeating. He was glad when someone shook him awake. "Oh, hey, Stella," he said groggily, looking up at all the party watching him.

"We're leaving," she told him.

"Don't we need to, like, gather supplies...?"

She shook her head. "Lucky bought and Violet bought what they could, but everyone in Ironspire is leaving. The shops that survived had a fire sale on everything. There's nothing left here."

James looked at her. "How long have I been asleep?"

"Long enough that I got _really_ tired of your snoring," Ralph said. 

"Figured." James got up and stretched. To be honest, having been woken up by Stella's screaming for several nights in a row, he wasn't sad that he had slept so long. He looked around and swore. The wreckage of Ironspire looked even more dire in the morning, with much of the horizon showing rubble and eyeball-craters. "Yeah. I see why everyone's leaving."

"We decided to go overland," Lucky said. "The canyon is blocked."

"Wait, _what?_" James asked.

"I overheard Paul talking about it---" Stella started.

_"PAUL!?"_ he about yelled.

"Quiet!" Violet hissed.

"He was passing by, talking to someone else," Stella pleaded. "Then I asked a shopkeeper. The Ironspire Mining Corporation decided to detonate the hauler that was stuck, but it accidentally closed off the pass. It's going to be a while before it heals."

"Before it _heals?_" James demanded.

"Oru physics," Lucky said. "The land just resets itself given enough time."

"Speaking of time, we should be on our way if we want to get out of here," Ralph said. "We're also taking a different route so we're not too close to the convoy. Lucky doesn't _think_ the Perfects can send another Titan, but we're better on our own."

He followed them on their way. James was about to argue that they should have woken him to consult him, but before he could even put a word to his mouth Stella said. "You were sound asleep."

"I needed it," James said. "You?"

"They let me sleep, too," Stella said. "No one wanted to wake me, and everyone else felt bad about waking you, too."

Yeah, they had all made the right choices. No use throwing a fit over it. Maybe it would be good to change the subject. "Two thirds of the way there," James told Stella.

"Close," Stella said. "More like 60%. We still need to refine the materials."

"Shouldn't be too hard," James insisted. Stella didn't answer.

* * *

The tablelands proved a significant obstacle to getting out of the ruins. Cliffs and canyons stood in their path wherever they turned, and were it not for Lucky having kept one of the maps she made for the caravan, they would have totally gotten lost.

Once they got atop one of the mesas, however, their journey was a bit more comfortable. Ralph warned them Slavers could be out there, but James personally thought that they would get fried by the whole party's fire spears.

Was that their next step, possibly? Raid a Slaver city for the remaining key fragment? He didn't mind the idea of killing some of those nurckers, but if there was anything the last forty-eight hours (or however the nurck the Oru counted time) taught him, it was not to be overconfident with Lucky's new magic.

They came to an incense tree. "Let's stop here," Lucky said. "This should increase the threshold beyond spider-riders, especially if I burn a blue scroll to cast _sanctuary._"

"Blue magic would be useful," Ralph mused. "Maybe more useful than red magic. We could get healing and shields."

"Maybe, but if you kill something before it can hurt you, you don't need to heal," James insisted. "Red is better."

"There are some offensive blue spells," Lucky said. "Rare, though."

"It doesn't matter," Violet said. "If we can find another one of those rings, then we can use it, but we just have Lucky's red ring."

"So it's that ring that gives you the power?"

They all turned to see that Paul, standing a few paces in behind of them, had spoken.
