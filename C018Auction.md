## The Auction

James watched in a very strange mix of satisfaction and distress at the clerk taking his old cert sheet and giving him a fresh one---one with "Level 1" clearly emblazoned under his name. Satisfaction, because he now could do far more. Distress, because he knew the more he got comfortable, the harder it would be to leave.

His next step, if he wanted to stay, was to purchase a slice of land from the Hold. Then he'd start making much more money, which he could use to buy a bigger slice, and one day reach Level 2.

If he wanted to stay.

* * *

That same day Tim approached him. "Hey, since you're a Level 1 now, I found out I could take you to one of those special events."

"Sure," James said. "What kind of event?"

Tim dropped to a whisper. "It's an auction."

"Of what?"

"I can't talk about it."

Well, it was worth seeing, at least. He didn't have much else to do. He wasn't good at chess, and he wasn't a writer who would enjoy the critique groups, and most of the other activities didn't interest him, either. What was the harm?

* * *

The outside of the auction house was one of the many fortified yet non-descript buildings towards the center of the Hold. In case of a last stand for the Hold, even confusing an attacking Trog Tribe or rival Hold increased the odds of survival just a little. Without knowing which buildings were irrelevant and which hid weapons caches, the defenders had a small advantage.

The disadvantage, James knew, was that any new citizen had no idea which buildings were which until you learned the layout. He had mentally dismissed this particular structure, large though it was.

When he stepped inside under the watchful eyes of a guard, however, he realized he had seriously misjudged why the outside was bland.

The interior was pure luxury, or as much luxury you could get in the Underneath. Elaborately carved wood interlayed with rare stones made up the walls, and the stage was similarly ornate. Taking one of the forty or so seats next to Tim, James found it was actually comfortable. The rest of the audience showed no less wealth, dressed in custom-made clothing that was dyed with rare materials.

Did the rest of the Hold have _any idea_ how much resources were dedicated to this place? James wondered. And then he realized why they didn't advertise it. Wealth made its own rules, even in the Underneath.

A server handed out white bowls of a greenish-blue fruit that James hadn't seen before. He knew rare plants existed, but either the Hold knew how to cultivate them, or they had enough not to care about handing them out for free.

Unless it was covered in the cost of the ticket. James decided he wouldn't ask Tim, though, and just nibbled on the fruit. It tasted of something other than meat or bluefruit, and was instantly delicious.

"Welcome, welcome," an snazzily-dressed man said on the stage. "I'm sure you've been eagerly awaiting the results of our trading with the Steathy Snakes..."

That had been a few days ago, hadn't it?

"We'll start off with a rare Spiderlily seed." A server brought forth the distressingly-shaped seed on another of those white stone bowls. "Starting at ten thousand Sunbucks---"

"Twenty K!" a fat Worker called.

"Thirty K," a thin Thinker called louder.

James's mind whirled. He had been paid ten Sunbucks a day as a Level 0. After taxes, rent, and everything else, he had about one Sunbuck of disposable income. The numbers they were throwing out didn't even make sense to his brain, and then those numbers grew higher.

"Two hundred fifty-six?" the auctioneer asked.

An expectant silence followed.

"Sold, to Bidder 25."

"Hold on to your money for later," a woman in a gown whispered to James. "They start off with something big, then a few items down they get to the _really_ good stuff."

James didn't even know what to answer. More items went by, seeds that went for inconceivable amounts of Sunbucks. Perhaps the Level 2s grew them in special, secret farms to resell for insaner profits.

"And now, for a blue firestone, starting at thirty thousand Sunbucks..."

The strange stone glowed with ethereal blue flames. A high-end crafting material, James guessed, although he hadn't crafted almost anything himself. It went for ten times the original price, and then the next material, a fragment of rare ore, went for double that, still.

Where did they even get this money? Then it hit James: the Trogs. As they would pass by, the rare items that the Hold supplied no doubt came from these people, who were appropriately compensated for their contribution.

But still! Was _this_ why Holds feared Trogs? As the items became enchanted weapons and armor that a Salamander would crave, James realized that every now and then the temptation to take by force what was too expensive to buy had to befall some Tribe. They didn't even need to annihilate the Hold, just rob them.

"And for our grand finale, a one of a kind Phoenix Sword _with_ a Swiftness enchantment. Starting at one hundred thousand---"

"---Two hundred!" the woman beside him eagerly said.

James stared, unable to process the insane weapon. Phoenix Swords would keep you alive even beyond your wound meter, although eventually with enough damage even they couldn't keep you alive. He had heard of them spoken of in whispers or tall tales, and the Dragon Trog Tribe didn't possess even one. For someone to have found or crafted one, and then risked an already priceless possession on enchanting it again...

"Five hundred! Five hundred!" the woman squealed in excitement.

Maybe this was the way forward, James thought. It would take a long time, but he could potentially find his route into Level 2 and then some means of getting these obscene sums, and then...

And then maybe forget about Stella after getting so much.

"Sold! One million two hundred forty Sunbucks." The crowd cheered at the happily shrieking woman beside James. "And that's all, folks. Enjoy the rest of your day."

The crowd of Level 2s didn't dissipate, though, but chatted with each other about their rare finds. James followed the excited woman until he had a chance to speak with her. "Worth it?" he asked.

"Oh, yes! It'll be the prize item in my collection."

"...Collection?" James asked, suddenly feeling even more disoriented.

"I already have two Phoenix Swords, and this is going to be so AWESOME everyone will be so envious..." the woman chattered on.

No, James realized, there was nothing for him here. The ultra-wealthy played their games with their play money and enjoyed their rare items as pure shinies, never to be used in actual combat. Maybe some of them thought about worldclimbing, but why should they? They already lived in decedance, prisoners whose cages were more comfortable than their peers.

He walked out without another word.

* * *

Back at his apartment, he sighed aloud as he sat down. What was left? Most Trogs just wanted adventure, with a sizable portion trying to worldclimb. The elites of Holds just wanted to play around. This left the Wayfarers, but what good were they? James knew a small party could never take on the hordes he saw in the Labyrinth.

What now?

His thoughts spun on end. He would have to leave, he decided. He reached under his bed... and found nothing there.

His equipment was gone.
