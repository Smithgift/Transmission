## Another Trial

James could tell by the intake of Lucky's breath that the recognition was mutual. Of course they might know each other. There weren't a lot of Arbiters or Perfects, and they all would end up knowing each other eventually.

The judge recovered and sat on his bench. But he stole another glance at Lucky.

"The case of Paul OCaml versus James Algol and party."

"Mr. OCaml, Mr. Solidity, if you would begin with your opening arguments," the judge said.

"May it please the court..." Paul began his story, which James admitted sounded so real it was almost convincing. According to him, he had found the ring after a Slaver attack on the caravan, and shown it to his former friends James and Stella. But, allegedly, he noticed that they never wanted to stay in the city. Eventually, after the Eyedra attacked, which they seemed to know about in advance (James almost exploded at that) they saw him use the magical ring to shoot numerous fire spears. They hunted him down after that, and though he pleaded with them to leave him alone, alas, they beat him up, took his ring, and left him to die.

Mr. Solidity showed pieces of Paul's armor with plenty of damage. "As you can see, there's clear sword and fire spear marks."

"Objection!" Mr. Root said. "You have not proved that that is Mr. OCaml's armor."

"I can wear it to show you," Paul said.

"Irrelevant. The question is not whether it fits, but whether it was your armor you wore at the time of the alleged assault. Any random Transmitted could take a piece of armor and smash it up."

James noticed the judge thinking. "Let's continue," he said. "With the proviso that the armor is not established as _the_ armor at the time."

Mr. Solidity frowned, as if uncertain why the judge would take such a stance on evidence so important.

Then came the party's turn. Stella started all the way back on Earth with the allegations, and uncomfortable interactions she and Lucky had had with him throughout their stay in Ironspire. "There is a ring, but _we_ found it in the boss jar of a dungeon," Stella said. "We... had to fight a lot of spiders. I'm... scared of spiders."

The judge looked sympathetic. "Continue."

"We also found a rare scroll that could summon green giants, which we used in the defense of Ironspire."

"Objection, irrelevant!" Mr. Solidity barked.

"Overruled," the judge said. "If the defendants fought the Eyedra, they likely didn't summon it."

Mr. Solidity looked strange again. James wondered why the trial was going so well their way.

"We did leave Ironspire by another route," Stella said. "Lucky here thought we should stay outside of cities or large groups in case monsters attacked. She's had really bad experiences with monsters spawning wherever she goes, so she wanted to stay away from people."

"Why? Why not safety in numbers?" Mr. Solidity asked.

"Because the number of monsters that spawn depends on the number of people in the area," Lucky said. "And we thought that if a Titan _did_ spawn, we'd be crushed inside the city walls."

The judge looked like he believed Lucky completely. "That does sound reasonable," he said. Mr. Solidity's jaw began to drop.

"Paul followed us, trying to take Lucky's ring," Stella said. "He also wanted to know how to use it, because he saw we could cast fire spears without effort using it. But she didn't want to teach him, because he had already mistreated and harassed her so badly, and then he _continued_ to do so. We barely got any sleep that night, because he kept screaming and whining and throwing a tantrum. Eventually, when he left, we heard Paul cry for help, but we couldn't just run into the darkness to find him."

"Oh, so you abandoned me!" Paul shouted.

The judge banged his hammer. "Mr. OCaml, you are out of order!"

"We believed you were dead!" Stella said, softer. "We didn't know you had a Phoenix Sword."

"You saw I had it! Why didn't you give a nurck and check?"

"May I bring it to the Court's attention that Mr. OCaml is now arguing as if Ms. Cobol's story was the truth?" Mr. Rust said.

Dead silence filled the courtroom.

The judge shook his head. "Mr. OCaml, if you were not attacked by monsters, why are you so upset that your opposing party did not help you with said monsters?"

Paul's lawyer hissed something in his ears, and Paul hissed something back. Time passed. James resisted, with his whole will, the desire to gloat. At last Paul said. "I misspoke."

"Ms. Cobol, is there anything more to your story?"

Stella shook her head. "We never tried to hurt Paul. We never even laid a finger on him. He's just wanted magic so much he's been harassing us since the beginning."

"I see." The judge said. Then closed his eyes.

Silence followed, James risking a smirk at Paul, and Paul looking furious back.

"What we have are two stories that contradict each other at every turn," the judge said philosophically. "But Mr. OCaml's story is inconsistent even with his own words. I judge the defendants not guilty, and I require that the plaintiffs pay all court costs."

"NO!" Paul practically screamed. "You don't understand!"

"Mr. OCaml, enough," the judge said. "If you---"

"Just give me nurcking magic!" he demanded.

"Mr. OCaml, please calm down," the lawyer said nervously.

Paul paused for one moment, holding his face. Then drew his Phoenix Sword out of his inventory and charged.
