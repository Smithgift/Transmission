## The Man

"What the nurck?" James demanded.

"Surely there must be some mistake!" Stella's boss protested, stepping in front of them. "Stella has been one of my hardest and most faithful workers!"

"I would like to see a warrant," Stella called firmly over the noise of the fragment-printer.

James looked carefully at the party of guards as the lead guard, a Watcher man, approached with a document. Their armor looked uniformly manufactured, and could very much be the top-level machined stuff that Ralph had talked about. If Lucky with him and Stella, they had no chance, but she wasn't, and in any case there were seven of them. He placed his hand on his sword, and they all stared him down.

Stella's boss read the warrant with increasing volume. "Assault, theft, _treason?_ This is completely ridiculous! Stella is---"

"It doesn't matter what _you_ think!" the lead guard barked over him. "We have orders to bring them in. Don't get in our way."

The boss's face flushed, but Stella held him on the shoulder. "We'll come. We're innocent, after all." She shot a Look at James, who begrudgingly nodded.

"Drop your weapons on your floor, open your inventories, and stand against that wall with your hands behind your head," the lead guard ordered.

Stella moved immediately to obey. When she opened her inventory, her boss immediately insisted that he take the Titan materials. "She came her for a business deal," he said. "If you don't like that, take it up with Mr. Pony."

"It's evidence!" protested a different guard.

"Is it in your warrant?" Stella asked.

The Watcher shook his head. "We are looking for a red ring."

James suddenly understood who was responsible for this dung. He pulled off his scabbard. "I can't open my inventory," he said.

"What do you mean?" the Watcher's eyes narrowed.

James tapped his nose. "It's a status effect. Can't do it."

"Whatever. Stand against the wall."

James felt anger course through him, especially when out of his peripheral vision he saw them pat Stella down. But she was right. They could prove their innocence.

Right?

* * *

Having been in a cell earlier in his Transmitted "career", he knew they didn't have much. No need for a toilet. No need for anything, really. Not even room. The hauler they put them in, handcuffs and all, held a bench at the back with the doors so tightly locked back it hurt his knees.

At least he was still with Stella. He leaned over and whispered. "We need to warn..."

"Ssh," Stella said. Yeah. As long as they didn't realize Lucky was out there, she and the rest of the party were probably safe. But if they all were locked up, while Vulkan was laughing maniacally about their helplessness... it made him sick.

"Nurck all of this."

Stella leaned into him. "We'll get out. Promise."

James didn't feel much better, but he leaned back.

* * *

The back of the hauler had no windows. James could only tell movement from the rumble of the machine, which stopped and started for no explicable reason, and could only sort of tell the passage of time. Sort of, because it felt like it took _forever_ for any time to pass at all.

He had no idea what boredom truly was before that moment. Stella tried to cheer him up with funny stories from back on Earth, but ultimately they both just sat there, quiet and very uncomfortable, as time sort of passed.

He didn't sleep at first, but time passed and nothing happened, and the adrenaline started to fade, he got tireder, and tireder, until at last sleep saved him from the unending boredom.

* * *

_Paul, riding a spider, was taking potshots at Stella, who kept screaming. Lucky fired fire spear after fire spear back, but never managed to kill him. Over and over and over..._

"Wake up! We're here!" Stella said.

James woke up to find Stella already awake and looking bizarrely cheery. A guard opened the doors, and James practically stumbled out from cramped legs. "Why are you so happy?" James asked Stella, as they both awkwardly hobbled out of the crawler.

"No monsters, no spiders," she said. "I had the best sleep of my life."

"I guess a dream is just a dream," James mused. He looked at the squat building, with bars over its windows. In the distance, tall smoothstone buildings, several stories high towered over the rest. "Where are we?"

"Gemstar, the capital of the Free Alliance," Stella said. "First among equals, really."

"Move along!" the guard said, and the two moved along.

* * *

They were separated shortly afterwards. They strip-searched him, and James had never felt more humiliated, then even more angry when he realized Vulkan had to be watching both of them. But what could he do?

When he finally got dumped into a cell, he paced about the cramped, almost empty room. First came anger, then he tried to calm down and think of a plan.

He still had his inventory. He might be able to escape and break them both out... but he knew if he did, they'd be on the run from the entirety of the Free Alliance. But what were his chances at trial?

Did they have trials in the Free Alliance? James had seen enough of the Scale recreated in the Tower of Worlds to know that the Transmitted still couldn't free themselves from it. If Watchers still made up the police, did they find some random Arbiter to run their trials?

Were they any better than those on Earth? If they were, James bitterly mused, then at least he'd be spared an investigation, because the Perfects rarely bothered interrogating anyone. Everyone knew the Arbiters just did whatever the Perfects wanted.

But no Perfects down here, except Lucky. How did the Free Cities pick their leaders? Did they have a democracy of sorts? James wondered how they did it, since no matter what they did, no one on Earth had experienced anything like that since the Collapse.

He sighed out loud, but couldn't think of anything. Time to still be bored. But if night fell, and Vulkan could scrounge together enough credits... they'd be trapped in here against a Titan.

* * *

But time passed, and nothing happened. When the guards came to his cell, James felt weirdly relieved. "This way," one said, and James followed to a room barely less cramped than his cell.

They chained him to a chain by the table. A Watcher in uniform sat on the other side. "James Algol, is it? We need to ask you a few questions."

James, for a moment, almost let loose on him. But the moment passed, when someone said from behind, with an immovable voice. "No, you don't."
