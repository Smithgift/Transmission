## Tensions

James marveled at how, merely one day into their travels on the Surface, they were already angry at each other.

James didn't know who blamed Lucky for what had happened. Maybe everyone, including Lucky, did. Maybe no one did. But he could feel the tension that had grown overnight. James found himself snapping at the others, and Violet had a brewing temper. Ralph's normally jovial dispensation had become saturnine.

And Lucky avoided eye contact with everyone else.

* * *

Their first argument had been over where to go.

"The farther we get from an arrival hill, the better," Ralph insisted.

"We need to find water, then follow it to a settlement," Violet equally insisted. "There's no way we can survive on our own like this."

"That's where the slavers put their traps."

"Yes, because the new worldclimbers have no other options."

"What do you think, Lucky?" James unwisely asked.

She shook her head. "I don't have an opinion."

"How? You're Lucky!" James pleaded.

"I don't have an answer for you." she said, not looking at him. "Sorry."

"Leave her alone, James," Violet said, and James only barely restrained himself from lashing out.

Eventually, for lack of any other plan, they decided to just keep going the way they were. James knew it would only be a matter of time before they had to sort out what to do for food. They had a bunch of 'reduce hunger' scrolls, but not enough to stay fed forever.

* * *

At some point the subject of the battle came up, and Ralph and Violet told their part of it. "We ended up on the other side of the Guardian from the door," Violet said. "We thought we'd smash the spawners, since we couldn't really help you two."

"Fred was the MVP," James said. "He held the line as long as he could. You, too, Lucky,"

"Thanks," she mumbled half-heartedly.

"How many flame spears do you use at the end?" Ralph asked.

"A lot," Lucky answered without looking at them.

"Wait a minute," James asked. "Where were you keeping them?"

"What do you mean?" Lucky asked, her voice rising a notch.

"Were all those scrolls in your inventory?"

"They're not in my inventory any more."

"Can I see your inventory?" James asked.

"What does it matter?" Lucky asked, her voice rising again. She started to tremble.

"You did agree to share your loot with the rest of us. That was the agreement," Ralph said.

"I have been," Lucky said. "Stop asking these questions!"

James opened his mouth, but saw the terror in Lucky's eyes, as if evil was about to occur, and occur to her.

Violet saw it, too. "It doesn't matter, does it? She's used those scrolls, and it killed a Guardian. We should just leave her alone."

"Yeah, I guess so," Ralph said. "Lucky, we do need to talk next time---"

"---it can wait until later," Lucky cut him off.

James regretted his words. They were already still angry about Fred, but they shouldn't be angry with each other. They needed to be angry about the Perfects who created this nurcked-up system.

And yet, James thought as he looked around. Did this place _really_ look like the work of the Perfects?

* * *

Lucky continued to act odd. She refused to talk to anyone, even Violet, and marched at some distance behind the rest of them. James thought about apologizing, but in truth, he didn't even know how he had hurt Lucky.

He probably should still apologize. He fell back a little. "Hey."

Lucky looked at him warily.

"Sorry for that incident."

She didn't respond for a few seconds, just marching on. "I forgive you," she said in a quiet voice.

James decided to march up ahead to rejoin the rest, just in case he stepped on Lucky's feelings again. Really, did it matter if Lucky was hoarding loot? It wasn't as if any of them would get any if it wasn't for her insane luck. And if she hadn't been hoarding all those scrolls...

Except, now that he thought of it, _where_ had she been hiding them? Scrolls would stack in the inventory, but had Lucky cleared her entire inventory of other items just to stock up on flame spears?

It didn't really make sense, either.

* * *

They ended up stopping at the next sundown, still in the same forest. James was glad there was still _something_ over his head.

The tension still hung over them, too, and James now wished he hadn't started that conversation at all. But there was no avoiding the next incident.

"Cut down _that_ tree," Lucky said, pointing to one that was taller.

Ralph shrugged, and started hacking at it with his axe. James, for lack of anything else, decided to look in search of brushwood. It was getting dark, though, so---"Ow!" His foot hit a log. "Hey, guys! There's a log already down here."

Ralph came over a moment later, and they eagerly hefted it up and brought to their camp. A moment from Ralph's knife and it was blazing. "That's easy," Violet said, as Lucky rejoined them.

"No it's _not!"_ Lucky suddenly erupted. "You need to cut down that tree!"

"Why?" Ralph asked.

"You just need to! That log won't work."

"It's just a log," James pleaded.

"It's the wrong _kind_ of log," Lucky said. "It's bad luck. Really bad luck."

"Listen, Lucky," Ralph said with patience-restrained irritation. "I know you have hunches but I've been up here, and I've never heard a thing like this log thing. Why do they work differently in the Underneath?"

"They just do."

"Lucky, what's different this time?" Violet asked in genuine confusion. "This is the same species we used the last time we struck camp."

"Fine, then," Lucky said, looking around as if the world could end any minute. She reached up and tore off her Unwanted bonnet.

Beneath, in organized waves of color, was the hair of a Divine-class Perfect.
