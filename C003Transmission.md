## Transmission

Dismantling his life had proven surprisingly easy.

Stella had to leave to dismantle her own life, knowing her conviction was equally inevitable. But when she left, James immediately started sending messages. There was no manual on how to do this, but he figured it out soon enough.

James didn't like his boss, so he felt fine about sending him a detailed list of all the flaws in his management skills. The Watchers might intercept it, but since his boss was actually just a Thinker, they might let it slide.

For his friends he just sent out a message in group chat. Two offered condolences, most kept quiet, and one asked him about his stuff. He told Mom to give it to the two that actually cared, if it wasn't valuable enough to sell.

He didn't have any other social circles, and the groups he lurked in wouldn't care. His account was already shared with Mom, since they had to work together financially. His books would return to the library automatically, but he decided to be a good citizen and return them immediately. He wasn't going to finish them anyway.

And then what?

He had to fill his time with _something._

He could binge watch his favorite shows, but he didn't want to spend his time so uselessly. Gaming was about the same, but he decided to spend an hour at it. After futilely trying to enjoy it, he at last lay on his bed and stared up at the ceiling, wondering what awaited him.

There was still a remote chance that he could be released if he got to the top world. Remote, but not non-existent. Or, if he met up with Stella in the Destination...

But James knew the Destination had to be huge. Or at least, he realized, he thought he knew. The Perfects had Transmitted such a huge number of people that if they were all stuffed into a small world, they'd run out of room. But he didn't know. Maybe there were giant writhing piles of people, all clawing their way to get out.

The thought disturbed him, and he couldn't get it out of his head. Then Mom knocked on his bedroom door.

He came out. "Yes?"

"I just... I just wanted to spend our last few hours together."

James went with her, and they indeed spent that time, laughing at old memories, joking, and crying, even James, for there was nothing more to be done.

* * *

Early the next morning he walked to the Tertiary Transmission Center. He could save Mom some money if he didn't take a lift or train, and he had nothing else to do.

He came to the large metal doors, labeled in bold print: TRANSMITTED ONLY: DOOR IS ONE WAY. He shrugged and walked inside, the first door opening to the second, and the second opening once the first slammed shut.

The pen inside---waiting room would not be putting it well, was already full of people, people crying, waiting, sitting, laughing, or raging. A few sat in a corner whispering to each other. Stella was nowhere to be seen---but her trial was today, not her inevitable Transmission.

He did look around the pen and counted hair colors. Lots of workers, but not a small number of Thinkers, either. A Watcher in jet blue hair watched from a corner---to be Transmitted, he realized, not to guard. A few Unwanted hung out in the opposite corner, bemused at the fate of their fellow Transmitted. After all, who knew how things would fare in the Destination?

A clock glowed above, but James watched tick through 0900 and keep going. Evidentally, they didn't care that much about keeping a schedule after all.

Every now and then an inner door would open, Watchers with pain batons at the ready, and grab a to-be-Transmitted seemingly at random. They tended to pick the nearest, James realized, or those causing the most fuss.

No bathroom, no water fountains, and not even a chair. This looked like it would be uncomfortable to stay in. So why bother?

He stood near the inner doors and waited. The next time they opened, they took him.

* * *

They had him strip, took everything, and washed him down with a spray of water from the walls. Another room dried him with heat. After getting a gown, they brought him to a doctor, who looked over him without sympathy until she found a scratch on his arm.

"When did this happen?" she grunted.

"Yesterday, when I came back from the trial. I---"

Before he could finish speaking she rubbed a strange pink powder on him, which burned. "Don't touch this for about ten minutes. They'll delay your Transmission until then, but if you pick at it, it won't heal."

"Uh, OK," James said. If the powder was what he thought it was---nanoheal, that pre-Collapse substance that the Perfects coveted, they had just wasted it on a man who was about to be zapped to another world.

Why?

The doctor didn't say a word for the rest of his examination, but eventually just said "Next!" The Watchers took him through the next doors.

He stood in a lengthy hallway full of walls with long, long drawers. Suddenly every nerve in him panicked. This was it. This was where---before he could bolt, they grabbed him and pulled him to an open drawer.

"In," a Watcher said.

James couldn't move.

"In!"

He struggled, but then they struck him with a pain baton and he nearly whited out from the pain. He clambered inside, and they shoved the drawer shut.

Inside was pitch-black, and he had no idea what would happen, let alone when or how. He wouldn't scream, he told himself. He would wait, wait, wait.

Ten minutes, the doctor had said. They'd delay for ten minutes. He had ten minutes to get out of here. He reached in the darkness for a handle or latch---anything! But found only a grate in the way for air.

He realized he'd brush off the nanoheal in the struggle. No. He would accept his fate. He would defy it to the end, but he would accept his fate. There was nothing he could do. Nothing that---
