## Caravan Attacks

Monsters, loads of them, spilled out of the wasteland. Even in the twilight, James could see the outlines of hairy two-headed giant humanoids with clubs and slings. James was about to jump into the fray. "Hold back!" Ralph called. "Protect the caravan!"

Indeed, the machines were already speeding along. James found their party running alongside it as the ettins chased them. Rocks flew overhead, missing James barely, but Transmitted fired back with arrows or spell scrolls.

An ettin stomped into the midst of them. James stabbed it in the thigh with spear, only for the ettin to stomp unconcernedly onwards. Ralph hacked at it with his axe, and it staggered. Another, better-equipped party got the kill with swords and better spears. James didn't complain, only picked up his spear.

"Run for the caravan!" Ralph called, and they ran to keep up. Even though the caravan's machines were slow, it was still better for them to try to keep a distance. One machine already had taken a direct hit to a side. They had to run to keep up with the fleeing machines, all while ettins continued to roar and chase.

Lucky threw darts, then ran out. Ralph pulled her along. "No time!"

And then, what felt like hour-long minutes later, the battle stopped. The ettins were gone, and a few braver parties went to loot their corpses.

"Nurck," a mercenary by them said. "Worst caravan attack I've seen in years."

James's party looked at Lucky, and she nodded grimly at their thought: the Perfects.

* * *

They did not get much sleep that night, not that they could. Everyone was talking about the battle, and a few parties had taken causalties. None of the machines were too badly damaged, aside from the one that had taken a direct hit. But it was still drivable.

"Lucky," James whispered. "Is it safe to keep doing this? They're going to send more monsters."

Lucky shook her head, then shrugged. "It's not _safe_ but we're low on options."

"You saw how our weapons weren't effective," Ralph whispered. "We're still safer in a group this size with parties better equipped than ours, then trying our luck in the wilderness. Even with..."

"Yeah," Lucky said. "We don't have infinite blue scrolls to burn."

"Can they send another attack of the same size?" Violet asked.

"If they have enough credits, yes," Lucky whispered.

Stella looked more and more disconsolate. James took her hand and squeezed it. "It's all going to be OK."

She shook her head. "I never liked fighting before and I really don't like it now."

"No option now."

She paused, but looked at him miserably and said. "There isn't, is there?"

James had a feeling the journey would prove her words true.

* * *

The second day of walking passed in a mixture of silence and muttered complaints about the previous night's attack. James felt sympathy for the other mercenaries, for whom it was merely some pitiless act of blind fate. No, for once the Transmitted were insufficiently paranoid or superstitious.

But there was no way he could tell them.

Lucky seemed disconsolate, and despite Stella's attempts to cheer her up, she tended to just walk in silence. Ralph shook his head when James mentioned it to him in a whisper. "Seen Lucky like this before. Think she's blaming herself for this."

James didn't know if it would be worth telling Lucky it wasn't her fault---especially within earshot of the other mercenaries.

Violet, on the other hand, had found a young man with the merchants. This Thinker had, in a mix of pride about his role in the caravan and an embarassingly earnest attempt to charm her, told her all sorts of things. The caravan had about four more nights before it reached Ironspire, and statistically (he assured her) the chances of another monster attack that large were minimal.

* * *

That night, the attack was even larger.

The spider-riders, on distressingly large spiders, weaved in and out of range; the spiders spitting acid and the riders firing from longbows or stabbing with spears. James's party ended up staying by the machines---without ranged weapons or extraordinary Speed, they had little chance of even intercepting them.

Stella screamed as one charged through the lines to them. James stabbed upwards, but did not hit the gremlin-like creature atop it. Ralph got a spider's leg, then Violet blasted it with a scroll. The spider collapsed, but before the gremlin could run, another party stabbed it.

The skitters and shouts of battle faded away as the spider-riders retreated. And then, silence.

Stella was hyperventilating badly. James cradled her head in his arms. "You're safe. They're gone. We won."

"I-I-I-*HATE*-spiders..." she cried.

"Stella," Lucky said quietly. "I know. They're going to keep using your fears against you. Blame them, not yourself."

Stella sat up straighter. "It---it doesn't matter. I have to become stronger."

Meanwhile, the young merchant went to Violet. "Are you all right?"

"We're fine, just shaken," Violet said. "But if you had any spare scrolls..."

Immediately the merchant pulled out his inventory and passed some over. "Don't worry about the price," he said. "Stay safe."

* * *

That next morning, Lucky immediately confiscated one of the scrolls that the merchant had given. "It's 'firestorm', one of the most powerful red spells," Lucky told Violet. "It's far too valuable to waste."

Stella, on learning _how_ they had gotten the scrolls, was a little upset with Violet. "I don't think we should play with his feelings like that."

"I wasn't, promise," Violet said. "I was just asking. He's the one reading romance into this."

Stella frowned. James put a hand on her shoulder. "You know how guys are. Always trying to score with the chicks. Besides, it's probably chump change to him."

"I know, but..." Stella started.

"Lucky, when can we learn any more spells?" Ralph asked quietly.

"Six days?" Stella asked before Lucky could answer.

"How did _you_ know?" Lucky asked, baffled.

"We had an enchanting process at the factory that always failed every eight days. They thought it was random until I started tracking it. We learned spells on one of those eighth days."

"Yeah, that's correct," Lucky said. "The stars align every eight days, in a sixty-four day cycle. The most lucky day we had back in the Labyrinth was sixty-fourth day, when white shines, and all magic works."

"You said that 'firestorm' spell was red? When's that?" James asked.

"Two cycles from now, so..."

"Fourteen days?" Stella asked.

"Yes, fourteen."

"Not soon enough to help us here," Ralph grimly mused.

* * *

No one slept the third night, except in brief watches. Everyone was waiting for the next attack.

It never came. The night passed without event.

"Just a statistical fluke, the last two nights," the bleary-eyed merchant told Violet the next morning, in the tone that one uses when trying to reassure himself.

"Thanks for the scrolls," Violet said.

"Oh---of course. Let me know if you need any more."

James questioned Lucky. "Why not last night? Did they run out of credits?"

Lucky shrugged. "I doubt it. But maybe they're nurcking with our minds, or maybe someone got called to a late-night meeting and wanted to watch it live. Vulkan was especially like that---he never wanted the rest of us to see something live that he couldn't."

 Stella looked up. "Vulkan?"

"Vulkan Lisp. He was my betrothed."

"That's the same Perfect who got James and I in trouble," Stella said.

Lucky's mouth fell open.

"To be honest, I didn't even look at the court papers," James admitted.

"I did, but they didn't have his name. It's just that he gave me his comms address in case I 'wanted out.'" Stella spat the last words.

"Great," Lucky said. "Absolute, nurcking great. He'll never let this go."

"It doesn't change anything," Stella said. "They'd still try to kill us all, no matter what."

"Yes, but he has a lot more resources. Let me... let me by myself for a moment." Lucky wandered off.

Stella watched her go. "Does she do that a lot?"

"Yes." Ralph shook his head. "Never knew why."

James had figured sometimes Lucky might have been practicing magic in secret. Or, now that they knew the Perfects were watching them, perhaps she was talking to her mother or her siblings who still loved her. But sometimes, James wondered, maybe she just walked off to cry.

* * *

The most dangerous part of the journey fell through a twisty high-walled canyon pass in the mountains, just before Ironspire itself. While it would not be impossible to simply climb over the hills, it would take days---in this wasteland where no food grew---and it would be impossible to bring the machines. The place would be perfect for an ambush, and James now knew the monsters, as skilled as the Slavers, could realize that.

Of course, it didn't take very much skill to realize that, which is why the captain of the caravan guards had had a shouting match with the chief merchant so loud that everyone heard either it or the rumors. The understandably superstitious mercenaries didn't want the next monster attack to occur in such a bad location, while the merchants insisted that it was mere chance, and if they hurried up, they could make it out of the canyon shortly after nightfall. It would never have even been an argument, James gleaned, except that the panicked escapes of the vehicles each attack had perversely driven them ahead of schedule.

Profit won over patience, and off they went through the canyon. There was not quite enough room for the guards to walk on the side, so most of the caravan either walked in front of or behind the machines. James wondered why it seemed the lower ranked mercenaries, like themselves, ended up in the vangard, until he realized that if the machines were to accerate in haste, they'd risk being run over.

"There's no way the Perfects will leave us alone," James said. "This is too easy."

"They'll wait until the time is right," Lucky said. "Or most entertaining. I'd bet they'll act right when we're almost---"

"Halt!" the guard captain called.
