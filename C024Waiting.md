## Waiting

James never got great sleep when on watch, so even after he had a chance to sleep, he was still bleary-eyed when the gates ground open.

"They're starting," Fred said.

"Do we want to watch?" James asked.

Ralph shook his head vigorously. "They might assume we're up to no good. We'll just have to wait."

"It's only going to be two and half hours before the gates close, anyway," Violet said. "We can check then."

* * *

So they waited. They didn't want to venture too far downwards in case they found a powerful monster and used up a scroll or salve, so they sat around and talked.

Fred had at least shut up about waiting, but James could tell he still didn't give up the argument internally. Lucky looked distant, as she often did. Ralph tried to cheer everyone up with jokes and tall tales. After a time of no one but him laughing he stopped. That left Violet and James to try unsuccessfully to talk about anything.

"What's the Surface like, anyway?" James asked. "I mean, what do people do up there?"

Ralph looked glum. "Fight each other, unfortunately."

"But why?" James asked.

"You heard of the War of Indenture?" Violet asked.

"No, what?"

"You might understand it better than most. You left Blue Sunrise because you wanted to worldclimb, right?"

"Yeah," James said, which was close enough to the truth.

"So you figure a lot of other worldclimbers are like us, right? Ambitious, ready for adventure, willing to brave danger and take risks?"

"What are you getting at?" James asked.

"Not enough food up there," Ralph shook his head.

"Not enough _agriculture_, to be clear," Lucky said, the first words she had spoken in a while.

"Basically, there's not enough food that grows naturally on the Surface, and most of the monster meat is inedible," Violet explained. "The only way for a large-scale settlement to survive is to farm heavily. Meanwhile, the machinery needed to produce the gear needed to survive up there is too heavy to be moved by nomadic tribes like the Trogs down here. So _someone_ needs to give up on adventuring to feed the settlements, and by 'someone' I mean the majority of the Surface's population. But no one wants to be that someone if he can help it."

"I don't know if the War ever had a beginning, but it's been going on for decades," Lucky said. "Some cities, the Slave Powers, raid new worldclimbers and the Free Powers to acquire slaves, who work their fields and service their machines. The Free Powers rely on a mixture of capitalism, voluntary sacrifice, and anger towards the injustice of the Slave Powers to have people work their fields willingly."

"I'm confused. How can these slave cities keep slaves?" James asked. "Wouldn't they just run away or commit suicide?"

"They're not _quite_ slaves," Violet said. "It's more like a harsher version of a Hold, where you gain rights over a series of years, and after that you're a new citizen. Some people are willing to be indentured as long as it takes to get free, rather than have to try to worldclimb again."

"Same difference in the end," Lucky said with sudden anger. "They just make sure that only a few of the most loyal 'indentured servants' ever reach their freedom. And then they make slave life tolerable enough that people give up on trying to change their fate. Well-treated slaves are still slaves."

James didn't know where the anger came from---whether Lucky had herself been one of the luckless worldclimbers who had gotten kidnapped. But she was an Unwanted. She had to sympathize with the lowest class more than any of the rest of them.

"People still try to worldclimb up, of course," Ralph said. "But considering that to do so---" He was cut off by the rumble. "Gate's closing. Let's look."

* * *

James had heard of people who tried to get psychiatric help on Earth. The Healers did officially treat it. But they also were reporting to the Watchers, so you could never really trust them unless you were struggling with something that did not involve the Scale. James had never gone himself. But he had read the word "PTSD" in the occasional contraband book or pre-Collapse entertainment.

He didn't realize what it was like until he saw the empty camp, and nearly bolted from the cavern.

No one shouted a warning. No one was even there to greet them. Piles of rejected gear and opened crates lay around in a far too familiar mess. If the Lions had tried to retreat, none of them had made it out.

"How can we tell if they've lost?" James asked.

"Lights will glow black on the door," Ralph said.

"Do we search the camp...?" Violet asked.

"If they couldn't use it we don't need it," James snapped with more emotion than he realized he had.

"He has a point," Fred said.

The runes on the door grew dimmer and dimmer. "Not a good sign," Ralph said. He shook his head as they flickered. "They've almost certainly lost at this point."

The runes turned completely dark, then back to dull.

"They didn't make it," Violet said.

James went over to a crate and kicked it, then drew his spear and started stabbing into it.

"James!" Violet said. "James, calm down---"

"THOSE NURCKING IDIOTS!" James screamed. "They should have... not done that." He fell to his knees, and listlessly speared the remains of the crate.

"James," Violet said. "They didn't know. There's no way they could have known---"

"Then the nurcking dungpit Perfects should have told them! *WHY?"*

Violet hugged him, and he started crying. At least tears worked in this nurcked up world with its nurcked up systems.

He just wanted to be home again, with Mom and Stella, and just... happy.

Not _HERE!__
