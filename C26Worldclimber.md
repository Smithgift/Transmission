## Part Two: the Surface

## Worldclimber.

The guardian them turned to ashes, then fell apart. With a horrendous rumble, James felt the ground shift beneath him like an express lift with the overrides disabled. But a moment later, the ground stopped, and a spiral stone staircase grew out of the chamber's center.

"We did it!" Violet cried.

"Nurck... it hurts..." Fred said. The monsters had chewed through his blackmetal armor, and much of his body glowed red or was simply missing. His remaining hand held tightly onto his Phoenix Sword. "Not... going to make it."

"Fred," Violet said firmly as she pulled out her inventory. "We have plenty of salves---"

"Not enough..." he said through grit teeth. "Hurts inside. Not sure how I'm still... alive. Just let me see... the Surface. Never going to..."

"Just hold still---" Violet started.

"Don't. You'll need it for yourselves. Sorry Lucky. Ruined your luck."

"You didn't," Lucky said. "It's just---"

"Less talking and more climbin'," Ralph said, and lifted up Fred by the shoulders. James picked the other end of Fred, which was disturbingly neither a full body nor a torso. James had once tried to lift a man who had broken a leg on the job back on Earth, but it took two people to barely drag him. But somehow---the bizarre systam at work---Fred was almost too light as they hurried up.

Lucky and Violet followed shortly behind. Despite all that had happened, James still could think: he had done it! He had worldclimbed! They were about to climb into---

...Another cave. It looked no different than anywhere in the Underneath. Even the luminescent blue moss was the same.

"Where to, Lucky?" Ralph asked.

"That way," Lucky said, and pointed down a tunnel. James followed as she let the way, her making marks on the walls, until they hit a dead end.

"What?" Fred groaned. "Bad luck, now?"

"Just keep turning left, that's the rule," Lucky said.

"Go fast," Fred said. "I'm not long for this world."

* * *

She hit another dead end, and the another. On the fourth branch, they finally came to an unbelievably bright opening in the cave, brighter still than dayshift lights in the tunnels on Earth.

When James stepped out, he almost dropped Fred from shock.

Above wasn't right, because there was no ceiling. The too-bright thing had to be the sun, or something very much like it, but it wasn't quite right either.  All around were real, leafy tree, not organized as in the hydroponics plants on Earth. Yet the trees weren't quite right either---their leaves weren't exactly right, he knew, although how he knew he didn't know. He felt both elated, and disturbed, and terrified.

"It's... it's enough," Fred said, his tense body from all the pain suddenly relaxing. "So this is what the surface... is. I saw it. I'll never see it again. 't's OK. Sorry for doubting you, Lucky." And then he tossed away his Phoenix Sword, and disappeared.

"Fred!" Violet shouted. "You idiot! We had enough---"

"Not if he had internal injuries, Violet," Ralph said, in a tone not unlike a father consoling an adult daughter. "You know salves don't work on those. And even if they did, we'll need the rest to survive here."

"But..." Violet held her face. "He could have at least let us say goodbye."

Strange guilt taunted James. He hadn't been able to say goodbye to anyone in the Dragons, even if their assault had been doomed from the beginning. He had said goodbye to Tim, but even then...

"We can sort this out tonight," Ralph said. "We need to get moving. The Slave Powers tend to patrol the arrival hills, and if we stick around..."

Lucky picked up Fred's old Phoenix Sword. "I've... got a little experience with swords," she said quietly.

"Hold on to it for now," Ralph said. "Move!"

* * *

They marched into the forest until sunset. No monsters appeared, but according to Lucky they didn't spawn during the day. Nor did they encounter any slavers. They just walked in silence through the brush, lost in thought.

When they did stop for the night, Violet sat with Lucky while Ralph and James hacked away at a tree. "Perfectly harmless," Ralph told him, and so it was: when the tree was felled, it split into two identical logs that tumbled peacefully to the ground. As if the Perfect's finger in the eye to remind the Transmitted, James mused, that even if they existed in a world like the destroyed surface of Earth, they still did not live in real life.

Then again, since he was effectively immortal and the Surface looked an awful lot like old Earth, maybe the Perfects had actually nurcked themselves over by sending their criminals to Destination. Those still on Earth had to suffer.

But looking at the exhausted faces around the campfire, all missing the jubilation of victory, James knew that there was no end of suffering to be found within these worlds, either.

"Just... one moment," Lucky said, got up, and disappeared.

"Lucky!" James called.

"Let her be," Ralph said. "She's suffering, too."

James didn't know what to say. In truth, while Lucky's call hadn't been a bad one---she was absolutely right about the difficulty being scaled---things had still gone very wrong. Had it not been for her burning through what must have been several stacks of 'flame spear' scrolls, they would all be dead, and then repeatedly killed afterwards.

Had Lucky's luck run out?

Or had it always been an illusion?
