## Lucky

Outside the gates, the usual tunnels and glowing moss of the Hold gave James a mixture of fear, deja vu, and oddly enough, comfort. They marched on as soon as they had their armor on, and James marveled at the amount of equipment the party had. Fred even had a Phoenix Sword. "Lucky found it," Fred said to James's admiring gaze.

"Yes," Ralph said. "We don't talk about Lucky in public. She needs her privacy."

"I see," James said, uncertain.

"Tell about yourself," Violet said.

"Well, I'm down here because a Perfect wanted my girlfriend..." James started.

"A Perfect would violate the Scale that badly?" Violet asked.

"We weren't even supposed to be together," James admitted. "She was a Thinker. But... we agreed to meet together in the second world."

"Well, let's hope we find her," Ralph said. "The Surface is a pretty big place, though."

"What's the second world like?" James asked.

"Big and wide open. You can see the sky, and it's weird," Ralph said. "I used to be a Worker on Earth's surface, too. Could never get used to the sight of the sky above."

"I was an accountant, actually," Violet said. "The Perfects needed me to cook the books, and I decided to so blatantly cook them that it was entirely obvious they were inaccurate."

"Why?" Ralph asked, shaking his head.

"Sick of the Perfects, to be honest. I couldn't stand being a crooked accountant any more."

"I'm like you, James," Fred said. "I had a girl, and she got Transmitted. Got myself Transmitted a few days later to come after her. Still haven't found her, though. Maybe that's OK."

"'Maybe that's *OK?*'" James asked.

"It's been close to a decade. I think she's probably moved on by now. Maybe I should, too."

James felt uncomfortable for the rest of the day's march.

* * *

They didn't bring food. As a single party, they could live off the land... or under the land, as the case might be. But they also brought plenty of _reduce hunger_ scrolls.

James tried one when they stopped for the day. He tore it in two, and it burst into red flames. The next moment, he felt significantly less hungry.

"Weird, isn't it?" Ralph guffawed.

"I feel like I've eaten, but I haven't," James said.

"I know, right? It's so weird," Violet said. 

"Anyway, try to stay off the scrolls," Fred advised. "We have a lot, but Lucky tends to find the better stuff."

"It's fine," Ralph said. "We didn't bump into any monsters today. Violet and Fred, you're on watch tonight."

* * *

James woke up to shouts of violence. "All clear," Violet said, panting.

Piles of troll meat lay around.

"Another horde while sleeping?" Ralph yawned, only mildly concerned.

"Nah, more of a party," Fred said. "We killed them all."

"Take the meat," Lucky said.

They obediently stuffed it in the inventory. "What about the hides?" James asked.

"Not worth it," Ralph said, shaking his head. "It just takes up space for more valuable items."

James felt uncomfortable, but said nothing. A Trog Tribe didn't waste anything... but he supposed a party of Salamander Wayfarers had different standards.

* * *

In the middle of the second day away from Blue Sunrise, they found the marks marking the limits of territory, and headed down the tunnels. The Hold didn't let other groups consume 'their' resources, even if they did not get much benefit from them.

"How far down have you been?" Ralph asked.

"Just the newt level," James admitted.

"Ah, well, if you're experienced with that, there's only a couple new types of monsters."

"*Types?*" James asked.

"Most of the monsters are simply stronger recolors of monsters in higher tunnels," Violet explained. "I know, right? Why spend so much effort to make this place and then copy and paste the enemies?"

"We ain't here for fun," Ralph rumbled, and they all fell silent.

Down on the lowest levels, the tunnels were thin enough that the party had to go single-file, though there was a strange amount of headroom. James was understandably in the middle as the weakest member, and he didn't argue---he felt nervous.

They stepped into a larger cave. "Bats!" Violet called, and James wielded his spear. Lucky threw darts and got several, and Ralph swung his bola and got another. James managed to spear one, and saw it had a singificantly incorrect number of wings. He shuddered.

"Let's keep going," Ralph said. "If you stick around in one place too long, you can get swarmed."

James nodded, and they headed on without a word.

* * *

The room's door looked like any other of the many rooms James had seen. But this one was much deeper in the Underneath, with the promise of much greater rewards.

Lucky and Ralph looked intently at the door. "Looks like a party slow if you throw something," Lucky said.

"I think so, too," Lucky said.

"You can read that?" James blurted.

"After you've opened enough doors, you start seeing the patterns on the weird symbols," Ralph said.

"Yeah," Lucky said. "Throw means release anything from your hand. So don't drop your weapon."

"Understood," James said.

Lucky pushed the door, and they charged in. A red troll roared in rage, but Ralph hacked at it with his battleaxe and chopped it apart.

"That was ea---" James started.

"Don't _say_ that!" Violet hissed.

"Sorry." James said.

James reached for one jar, but Ralph grabbed his arm. "Only Lucky opens jars."

"Really?" James asked. The reason Trogs rotated jar opening was because equipment found in a jar would always be the size of the person who opened it. It also gave a small amount of Trickery exercise, so it was worth spreading it around.

"Yes, really," Violet said.

Lucky inspected a jar, thought about it, then inspected a different jar, rapping on it a few times. Then she opened it, to get a sword that blazed with green fire, a tower shield, three scrolls, and a glowing stone.

"What the _nurck?"_ James asked, as Lucky looted another jar, with similarly insane loot.

"I don't know how she does it, but Lucky is _the_ luckiest Transmitted in all the Underneath," Ralph said.

"And don't tell others," Fred said. "We're worried people might try to kidnap her if they knew."

Lucky nodded, gently. The third jar was more normal stuff, along with a white bowl, but the fourth contained a bunch of salves and the fifth more glowing stones.

"I guess I'm now a believer," James said, watching in awe.

Lucky paused on the fifth jar to look back at him with an strong yet undecipherable look. "Did you ever want an Unwanted?"

James shook his head. "I mean, it's not like I hated them, but... sorry for whatever you've gone through."

"I get it," Lucky said. "Let's keep moving."

* * *

That night in a higher cavern, James and Ralph kept watch. Lucky got up, stretched and wandered off. "Where are you---" James hissed, but Ralph put a hand on his shoulder.

"Lucky likes to be alone sometimes," Ralph said. "We leave her be. Just between us, something must have... you know, she doesn't like being touched, and she doesn't like being too close to people."

James didn't answer. He knew all too well what might have happened to an Unwanted back on Earth. It was a shame, how they became that way: just something went wrong and they didn't have a hair color listed on the Scale.

But what difference did that make? They hadn't done anything at birth to deserve anything more or less.

And yet, James realized, he hadn't really seen Lucky as an equal. Even though down here, and at least outside of the Holds, the Scale no longer existed. But maybe, just maybe, it was still cruelly carved into his heart.
