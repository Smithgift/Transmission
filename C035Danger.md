## Danger

"What?" James asked.

"I can't explain here," Lucky said. "But we need to leave this city before nightfall."

"Why? Wouldn't we be safer behind the walls?" Violet asked.

"Not unless you want everyone inside the walls to die, too!"

"What?" Stella asked in confusion. "Who are you?"

"Lucky knows a _lot_," Violet said. "We can't tell you why."

"I'll tell you why when we're out of here," Lucky said. "But we need to leave _now!_"

James looked around. The passerbies peacefully walked the streets and chatted with each other, and from the inside of the tavern laughter roared and dishware clattered. Nothing _seemed_ off but the terror on Lucky's face. And that terror was real.

"I have... stuff I need to pack," Stella said.

"If you can't pack it up tonight, leave it," Lucky said without hesitation.

"Will it be dangerous if we split up?" James asked. "Then we can get more stuff done---"

"Ironically, no," Lucky interrupted. "But meet at the west gate. We need to be in the forest while there's still light enough to see."

"Let's get moving," Violet announced. "Ralph and I can get food, and you two can pack. Lucky---"

"I'll be with you and Ralph," Lucky said. "It'll be a bit safer."

James took Stella's hand as they ran back towards her apartment. "James," Stella said. "Do you trust these people?"

He thought of it only for a moment. "With my life."

"OK," she said, and with unspoken agreement they ran faster.

* * *

James could see Stella had settled in, buying knickknacks and decorations and things that were not for mere survival. But Stella ignored them as she dug under her bed for a large box. She pulled out quality gear, but gear clearly made in the Underneath. "I thought I'd never have to adventure again," she said.

"Do you know where the rest of your Tribe is?" James asked.

"They left without me. I was OK with it. I told them I was waiting for you. And then this happens---whatever it is! Do you have any idea?" Stella strapped her sword to her hip.

"No," James said. "But Lucky's almost never wrong."

"'Almost never?'"

"Close enough to never to that we know she isn't joking when she says our life is in danger."

Stella looked at him with determination. "Then we'll make it through. We're in this together."

"We are," James said, and kissed her on the cheek.

They hurried out. "I just thought we had found happiness," Stella said, her voice wavering towards tears.

"Stella, I thought so, too."

* * *

The bank had a full line of ordinary customers going about their ordinary days, all waiting for the one teller at only open window. Furniture luxurious for the Tower of Worlds awaited any who needed a calming moment or to peruse the financial statements on the various desks. Windows showed equally ordinary people coming and going outside on their daily errands.

Once again James felt dislocation: nothing seemed even slightly amiss, but Lucky _had_ to be right. But what? Was this why she wasn't so excited about searching for Stella?

James and Stella got in the end. "I have a lot saved up," Stella said. "We can at least buy some better gear. Or maybe some salves."

But the line slowly ground forward, as one customer bickered with the teller, then another, then the next. James felt antsy, as if he should shout _You're all in danger, right now!_ But what could he prove, beyond that an Unwanted they didn't know was a Perfect was warning them?

James waited, but the next customer was at the verge of shouting at the teller before an armed guard tapped on him on his shoulder. The customer stomped away with a look of loathing at the teller. "James," Stella said with worry. "It seems that _everyone's_ angry."

James looked around. No one seemed panicked coming up, so it didn't feel like a bank run. But why did they leave so angry?

When their turn finally came, James soon found out. The teller, an old Worker woman, glared at them as if the two were personally responsible for ruining her day. "What do you want?" she practically spat.

"I want to withdraw my money," Stella said, baffled.

"Fine. Let me get your account. What's your name?"

"Stella Cobol."

The teller looked in a filing cabinet, breathing threats and imprecations that were not quite inaudible. "What's the name again?"

"Stella Cobol."

"What?"

"Stella Cobol!" Stella practically yelled.

"Fine," the teller muttered, and looking through the cabinet, finally pulled out a thick folder. She shuffled back to the desk. "How much do you want to withdrawal?"

Stella looked at James, who didn't know what to reply. Stella turned back. "All of it."

"What. _All of it?_" the teller said.

"Yes, all of it, please," Stella said.

_"Why?"_

"I'm leaving this area."

"Oh, so you think you two are going to succeed worldclimbing where no one else has---"

"Excuse me," James interrupted, restraining his anger. "This is Stella's money---"

"Stop talking when someone else is talking!" the teller yelled. "No one respects their eld---you nurcking Unwanted! That's not where the line starts!"

Lucky had slipped down the line to them. "James, we need to get going within the next half an hour---"

"Out! OUT!" The teller barked at Lucky. Lucky hurried away. "Where was I? You want to withdraw all your money? You realize that you're at risk for..."

"Please," Stella said. "Just let us withdraw _some._"

"How much is 'some?'"

At that point, James lost his temper. "What the nurck are you doing? This is worse customer service than I got from an ATM."

That apparently also was the last straw for the woman. "OUT! _OUUT!!_ AND NEVER COME BACK!" she practically screamed, and the two hurried out.

* * *

Lucky was waiting, even more concerned. "We don't have any time left. This way---"

"But I need to---" Stella started.

"You need to get out before it's too late!"

Stella followed with a frown on her face. If it hadn't been for that teller's cruel attitude, James realized, they could be far more equipped. But Lucky broke into running, and the two followed her. In the street intersections, torch-bearing men and women with official clothing were lighting the fireplaces.

When they reached the west gate, they saw an ongoing argument between the guards and Ralph. "People come and go all the time," Ralph was saying patiently. 

"Not at nightfall you don't," a guard said.

"Please, there must be some misunderstanding," Stella said. "We just want to leave."

"But _why now?"_ the guard said. "It's awfully suspicious, isn't it?"

"It's part of our religion," James said calmly.

The guards looked at him with a strange expression, as well as Stella and Lucky with an even stranger expression.

Stella started to speak, but James continued, in a confident tone. "We can't talk about it to outsiders, but there are certain rituals we can only do at nightfall in a forest. Secret sect, can't talk about the details of course."

"There are things that just can't be explained to outsiders who don't follow the way," Lucky said, adding a slight emphasis to the last words.

The guards conferred. "I mean... we do have rules on respecting religious liberty," the skeptical guard admitted.

"If it's the group I'm thinking of, they're totally harmless," a guard said. "Most of the time, at any rate."

"Well, if you finish with your ritual before nightfall, you can come back in," the skeptical guard said. "Other than that, you're on your own." The guards hurried them out.

"Best of luck with it!" the less skeptical guard called from behind.

Off they went as the sun drooped lower and lower. "I... I wish you hadn't lied," Stella said.

"There's a bunch of secret cults out there," Lucky said. "Maybe that guard was in one of them."

They walked on. Stella shook her head, as if deciding not to argue it further.

"It's going to be OK," James said, wrapping his arm around Stella.

As they ran into the forest, Stella whispered in his ear. "James, there better be a really good reason for this," Stella said.

"Lucky's never wrong about the Tower of Worlds," James said.

"The what?"

"Just trust me, she knows."

"OK," Stella said, and hurried along.
