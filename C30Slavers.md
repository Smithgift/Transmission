## Patrols

As they stepped closer, James noticed there were only five of them, and they didn't have any kind of insignia on their armor. Slavers? Or a genuine patrol?

"Let me see," a young Worker woman said, kneeling by Lucky. "Can you show me?"

Lucky yanked down her bonnet. The other party froze for a moment. Ralph sliced off one's head. The young Worker woman didn't have a chance to react before Lucky stabbed her through the throat. James reacted just as the enemy leader did. They speared each other. The leader's spear caught James's arm in the shoulder, but James kicked him back in the nuts. The leader bent over in pain, and Ralph chopped off his head.

Violet had been tangled by the spears of the two others, but had her inventory open. She pulled out and ripped a scroll in half and killed one instantly. The other tried to escape but got killed by Lucky's precision dart to the eye.

And then the forest was silent, once again.

"Please tell me we actually killed Slavers," James pleaded.

"Yes. But we'll find out for sure in a moment," Lucky said, as she rummaged through the loot. "The 'we're a town patrol, just trust us', is one of the oldest tricks in the books. Second only to the helpless young woman separated from her party and the mysterious abandoned camp."

James didn't argue that that meant the patrol trick was third, not second, as he was still pretty amped up from the fight. "At least we have their gear," he said, admiring the loot piles.

"Don't take it," Lucky said. "It looks Slaver as nurck. Here!" she pulled out a map.

They crowded around it. James didn't know how it possibly could have been created except by magic, because it appeared aerial. But the arrival hills were circled and notes indicated the paths of actual patrols. Other marks showed a path across a vaguely definied border and the Free cities.

"Found their compass," Violet said, digging through the same pile of loot. She tapped on it and strange base-8 coordinates appeared above it in glowing text. "We can find our way to the nearest Free town from here."

"How will we know if they're not Slavers?" James asked.

"The first thing they'll do is demand proof that _we_ aren't Slavers," Lucky said. She carefully redid her bonnet. "They'll do this by attempting to intimidate us, then call for backup, then interrogate us with a bunch of questions to see if our story holds together."

"But can't Slavers do all the same?" James asked.
 
"Yes." Ralph said. "And they sometimes do. But we get to interrogate them a bunch to make sure they're not, either. Either way, don't let them surround us."

* * *

They didn't try to bury the "remains" of the Slavers, or even try to acknowledge that they had killed anyone. Off they went, following the map. James wondered if the Surface was so treacherous that every party ambushed before they were ambushed themselves, but at least the rest of his party seemed to know what to do in these situations.

He had trouble sleeping that night, though. Even if they had been Slayers, they had killed other people and sent them back to the death loop of the Underneath. At least the smell of Lucky's special logs...

"Monsters!" Violet shouted. James reached for his spear and had a moment to react before the creatures were upon them. Strange horned beasts and misshapen ants and---he had no time to think, only to stab and fight, and---

Then it was over.

"Nurck," Ralph said, wiping his eyes. "I hate night ambushes. Why didn't the fire stop them?" he asked Lucky.

"Everyone, check your trees," she said grimly.

All of their black *hamartia*-trees had grown significantly. "What did we do?" James asked. "We killed some evil Slavers!"

"Chances are they either had high _arete_, and the system thinks we shouldn't have killed them, or it was smart enough to detect the treachery," Lucky mused. "Or maybe some other reason. The point is, the incense logs aren't perfect."

"Great," James said. "Absolutely, nurcking great. The system thinks we're the bad guys now."

"Bad in the eyes of the culture that made this place," Lucky said. "Not _bad_ bad."

"We would have died, or far worse, if we hadn't ambushed them," Violet said.

"Check for wounds," Ralph said. "And I think we're all going to stay up tonight, until we find some way to fix our trees."

James pondered Lucky's words for the rest of that sleepless night. On Earth, the Scale was said (by the Perfects) to be the ultimate arbiter of good and evil, and the optimal system for the Rebuilding of Earth's surface. Lucky seemed to have extraordinarily strong opinions on right and wrong, though, and she was, if exiled, still a Perfect.

Something else she said struck him as odd, but by the time he started to think of what it was fatigue had finally put him to sleep, threat or no threat.
