## Blue Sunrise

Just like that, James was once again alone.

He considered sitting down and crying. But suddenly an anger he didn't realize he felt flooded through him. Anger at the Perfects for their tyranny. Anger at the Perfects for sending him here. Anger at this twisted system that tormented them.

He took a deep breath, and looked through the fallen equipment. He put the best in his inventory, wished he knew what to say, and walked on.

* * *

James didn't actually make it to Blue Sunrise before one of their scout parties found him in a nearby tunnel. They wore decent gear, as far as James could tell, but nothing Trog-quality. James still knew he couldn't take them if they attacked him.

"Who are you, and why aren't you with your Tribe?" their leader, an old Worker man asked.

"Tribe's gone," James said. He found the grief that he had so far kept at bay start to leak into his voice. "We were wiped when fighting a Guardian. I'm looking for somewhere to stay."

Their leader shook his head. "Sorry to hear that, but we need proof you aren't a mole trying to get behind our gates. Understood?"

"Ask Tim Ruby," James said. "He knows me."

"I know Tim," another Holdsman piped up. "He's the guy who's always sleeping by the fires."

The leader looked at James with slightly less suspicion. "This way," he said.

James followed, but he could tell they were watching him.

* * *

When they arrived again at the gates, one of the scouts played a whistle, and they advanced a bit closer. "Stay right here," the leader said.

James waited. He had nothing else to do.

A few minutes later, Tim came out with a group of other Holdsmen, overjoyed. "James! You're alive! I was so worried!"

James found himself hugging him. "The rest of us didn't make it."

"You're not going to try again, right?" Tim looked at him with wide eyes. "Right? You're going to stay with us, now?"

"On that subject," a Thinker woman said. "We're willing to let you in, but only if you agree to join our Hold."

James thought about it. He _could_ refuse, but even with his gear and some experience being alone in the Underneath was very likely death for him. He could stay for a while, get prepared, and head back.

"I will," James said.

"This way, then," the Thinker woman, said, and James followed her to the gates. The woman played a flute, and they opened.

"You sure like your... music..." He trailed off at the sight before him.

James had been expecting more of a small fortress with a garden. What he saw were massive farms stretching across almost the entire cave floor, with fruit of every color growing in field after field. Huts were stationed everywhere, each with additional fortification, while in the center of the Hold was another defensive structure.

If the Holds were afraid of a Trog attack, this Hold at least would have needed to fear nothing from the Dragon Tribe, for they had practically their own world down here. Maybe a concerted effort from several large Trog Tribes.

Or did they fear war from another Hold?

James marveled at the scale of the fields. Every last space they could possibly fit a plant had a plant. Bluefruit grew along the ground, while yellowfruit stalks grew among them. Elsewhere, trellises of redfruit filled the empty space. In one section stood organized rows of trees, intermixed with stumps. "Quite the operation you've got here," James managed.

"You'll be assigned to the farms, to be clear," the Thinker lady said.

"Understood," James said. It would be temporary, he knew. But he didn't have any other option right now.

* * *

James hated paperwork, but thankfully the Trogs tended to eschew it for the sake of efficiency. The Holds, on the other hand, seemed to think the more papers you filled out, the closer you were tied to them. James found himself signing and filling out so many documents he felt like he was back on Earth. One was a lease for an apartment, and another was an overview of the entire laws of the Hold.

James normally read documents before signing them, but the Thinker woman in charge of new arrivals kept glaring at him. He ended up signing them fast. He didn't have much option, in any case.

He noticed the Holdsmen all tended to eye his gear. And he also noticed that almost no one was equipped with any weapon. Those who did tended to be Workers or even Watchers.

"You'll need to turn in your gear," the Thinker woman said. "We don't allow ordinary citizens to be armed."

"It's mine," James protested.

"I'm sorry, but you're a citizen now. You have to turn it in. You will be compensated."

"Fine," James said. "Give me a moment to change."

"Just turn them in by roll call tomorrow."

* * *

James found his new apartment small but definitely comfortable. It had a bed, a desk, and a chair. Nothing else, but definitely better than the Trog Tribe's dormatories.

He took off his armor, but hesitated. He would need them later. He dug through his inventory and emptied it, but put some of his best gear back in his inventory.

There. He could give them the rest and pretend that was all of it. They probably wouldn't even notice it wasn't the original things he was wearing.

He undid the Trog bandana at last. The Dragon Trog Tribe was officially disbanded by the last member. He stared at the piece of cloth, and hid it under his bed.

He would not forget them.
