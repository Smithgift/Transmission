## Transmission

The phrase still hit him in the gut, even if he had expected it. The Perfect meanwhile, looked on with glee. The Perfects in the audience clapped at "justice" being served.

"You will receive an email with your date and time for Transmission. If you do not arrive at that time, you will be executed. Do you understand me?"

"Yes, sir," James said.

"Court dismissed."

As he walked out, the Perfect followed and came up right next to him. "Her trial is tomorrow. Unless she wants to _opt out._"

James was tempted to hit him. But he ignored the Perfect and left.

* * *

They did not waste time. He had already received the email when he checked his comm when he got back.

> To James Algol
>
> You are scheduled for Transmission at 0900 at 2213-04-02, at the Teritary Transmission Center in Shaft 14. Herein fail not.
>
> Johnson R
> Transmission Scheduler.

"How did---" Mom started, but stopped when he showed her the message. She groaned a cry, then hugged him tight. "Why? How could you do such a thing?"

James thought for a while. At last he said. "Because I loved her."

Mom sat down, holding her face. James knew what must be going through her mind: in addition to losing him, she would have one less income coming in, and ever since Dad died in a work accident they had been struggling.

"Mom---"

"Don't cry," she said. "Don't worry. We'll get through this. Somehow."

James didn't share her hope, but right then he wanted nothing else. He sat with her for a bit, and then couldn't take it.

He went to the fridge and cracked open one of the pre-Collapse sodas they had bought as an investment. He poured it into two cups, then handed one to Mom. She drank it without a word.

James drank the other. He could tell why they were once so desired that no one could know the secret recipe, lest more be made without the consent of the maker. For before the Collapse, private companies did whatever they wanted, not controlled by the Scale.

The Scale that had so cruelly treated him.

There were always a few crazies who tried to overthrow the system. But they never succeeded. The Perfects controlled too much, and the Watchers and their electronics were everywhere. So he just suffered.

But not for much longer, theoretically.

He brought up the Noctypedia article on Transmission. The world that criminals and the very unwanted Unwanted were send to was less one world and more several worlds, stacked in a tower. But how to climb them, no one in this world knew except the Perfects. After all, if you reached the top... you would be released.

No one had ever managed it, according to the unofficial sources. But if there had been, surely the Perfects would be celebrating, to prove that their strange system of reformation was effective as it was claimed.

The knock at the door caused them both to look up. But the door wasn't hammered on or kicked down, which meant it was probably a friend. He opened it to find Stella, without a bonnet, waiting for him. "I heard," she said.

He let her in, and they sat together at the table. "I'd offer you a pre-Collapse soda---" James started.

"You mean this?" Stella said, pulling out a whole bottle.

They all stared at her.

"It's not going to be much use to me in a few days," she said. "Unless I accept... a certain devil's plea bargain."

She poured it and they had more. Allegedly, the Perfects had their own special soda that was also alcoholic, though even the species of plant that could make it was extinct from the Collapse. For those in lower classes, you had to mix it yourself.

"I brought some... information," she said. "I'll tell it to you in private."

"OK," James said.

They hurried to his room, and she pulled out a tablet. "There are eight worlds in the Destination. Apparently they're filled with monsters and traps, and something called Guardians that keep the gates between the worlds. But here's something they try to keep secret."

"Yeah?"

"There's some strange... system? The sources I have weren't clear. But involves numbers of some sort. I heard it described as a kind of really, _really_ strange RPG."

James had enjoyed his share of games when not working, since they were free and even the Perfects would indulge in them from time to time. But perhaps they enjoyed it even more than that? "What's strange about it?"

"Apparently none of the systems actually make any sense. But the source I have refused to say one word more. He said he only told me because I was going to get Transmitted myself."

"Huh," James said. "Maybe they want it to be a puzzle."

"I don't know. Also, apparently you can't die for real. You just respawn---but you always respawn on the lowest world, and you don't get to keep a single thing from your earlier life."

"Weird. I'd think it'd kill you for good if it was supposed to be a test."

"I'd think so, too, but my source swore up and down that's how it worked. And..."

"What?"

Stella shook her head. "There's something to do with how much the Perfects know about the worlds. It's... I don't know how to put it. My source didn't want to talk about it all, but he knew _something."_

"OK."

"And that's it," Stella said with a sigh. "That's all I know. I just wanted to spend what could be either of our last days together."

James wrapped an arm around her. She wrapped an arm around him.

"Do we want to..." James trailed off, unable to ask to cross that _particular_ line.

She shook her head. "There's a secret I can't even tell you. But for the moment, we'll just be with each other."

"Even if there's never any more?"

"Even if."

They held each other.
