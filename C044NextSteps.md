## Next steps

"A key fragment?" Violet asked.

"Yes, and it's already been refined," Lucky put it in her inventory. "Tell no one. We're a third of the way there."

James suddenly realized how insanely generous the captain had been. "I thought the Slaver leaders didn't leave their cities," James whispered.

"Evidently this one did," Ralph said, shaking his head. "Maybe he wanted to worldclimb, so he was off to get more resources and slaves."

"I don't think these guys were after slaves," Violet said, and tapped a fallen sword with her foot.

A worried noise from Stella made James turn: she had her destiny screen out, and both her trees were now colossal. James checked his to find the same.

"The Perfects?" Violet asked.

"Yes, or the fact we just killed plenty of monsters and people," Lucky said. "Sometimes we'd pump everyone nearby full of _hamartia_ so we could send more and bigger monsters."

"Great," James said. "Do you think people will mind if we gather up some loot?" He looked eagerly around at the fallen Slavers' gear.

"Let's," Ralph said. "If we killed it, it's ours. That's the rule."

* * *

It took well into the morning before the caravan was ready to move again, between cleaning up the battlefield, fixing the haulers that had been damaged, and the inevitable need for humans to celebrate the victory. 

They also had time to mourn, briefly. The mercenaries had taken plenty of casualties, and so had the actual employed guards and the merchants. Unfortunately, the young merchant who liked Violet had gotten killed---who knew by what---but Paul had made it. "Last time I'm on one of these caravans," he muttered beside Stella as the haulers moved on.

That had also been a good thing: James's party ended up on one of the haulers itself, riding the unpleasant bumpiness as the caravan made the final stretch. He looked around: Ralph seemed sober, Stella distressed, Violet exhausted, and Lucky looking around with her usual weary expression. James himself felt relieved that they had all made it, especially considering the injuries. They couldn't talk about their next steps, however, because Paul was there.

James didn't like how close Paul wanted to sit next to Stella.

"Where you heading off after this?" Paul asked.

"Probably not another caravan trip, either," Violet said.

"Figured." He stretched. "Know some good places in Ironspire to crash. Know some real dungholes, too."

"Hey, man," James said at last. "We're going to do our own thing, OK?"

Paul shrugged, but didn't answer.

The caravan rumbled underneath them.

* * *

James had expected Ironspire to have, well, a _spire._ That was sort of true, in that there was an large peak that had numerous mining shafts into it. But it wasn't really a spire. Nor, according to Stella, was the material they mined really iron---it did not exhibit any of the metallurgic properties of Earth iron, despite being named the same thing. But still, if you were going to name a mine, Ironspire seemed pretty metal.

"They say those shafts were already there when people came here," Paul said. "Wonder who put them in. The Perfect's game designer?"

Violet had the look of someone who was trying her hardest not to speak, and Lucky blanched.

"Anyway, if you change your mind and want me to show you around, just head over to the Raw Horse. They don't serve raw horse, by the way. Just their name." He walked off.

"He's too smart for his own good," Lucky muttered.

"I'm... I'm a little nervous around him, too," Stella admitted. "He wasn't like this... earlier." She gazed off as if in thought.

"Let's get going," James said. Everyone got up and followed him.

* * *

The outpost looked like someone had sincerely tried to build a stereotypical fantasy mining outpost, but had run into difficulties that this world did not supply the right materials. Everything was made of smoothstone, and the buildings more ramshackle than quaint. Miners walked to and fro---the mining never ceased. Nor did the fires, for sometimes monsters would wander out of the mines, seeking to destroy.

The moment Paul was out of eye shot, Ralph muttered. "Let's keep outside city walls. Even if the Perfects wouldn't send a Titan, they could sent something like that stream of monsters on the caravan.""

"Agreed," Lucky said. 

So they headed out some distance from the walls and sat on the sandy rock.

Stella counted their pay. The quartermaster hadn't been able to raise their base pay from the 70 he had promised them ("stupid corporate reasons" he had muttered) but he gave them all a nice bonus, so together they had around five thousand freedollars. That wasn't rich, but it also wasn't poor.

They had also nabbed weapons from the fallen Slavers, but the armor tended either to have holes from the shadow bats or just the wrong size. James found a glowing pair of boots, however, and he found he could jump pretty far with them.

"Now what?" James asked the question they all had.

"Now we make a bunch more money and buy the other two key fragments," Lucky said.

"I had a thought," Violet said. "Stella, you know a lot about how enchantments work, right?"

"I know some, yes," Stella said. "We were doing plenty of research even when I left."

"And Lucky, you know a lot about the Oru theology of this all."

"More or less, yes," Lucky said.

"So why not use your 'luck' to get rare items, then you and Stella can enchant them with powerful enchantments? Even if you mess up some times, you could still get some pretty powerful stuff to sell."

"Powerful, but not top-end," Lucky said. "That's only manufactured from rare materials."

"It'd be academic for most Transmitted," Ralph said, hand on his chin in thought. "The armies use standardized equipment, but no adventurer is going to say 'no' to a highly enchanted rare drop."

"We could also just use it for ourselves," James said. "We need to be stronger."

"That, too," Lucky agreed. "I can't guarantee any particular drop, but with my _arete_ alone we should be getting rare things."

"All in favor?" Ralph asked.

All hands raised.

"We have a plan," Ralph said, satisfied.

"If I may, we should take this day to rest," Stella said. "We didn't get any sleep last night, and I don't want to go into a dungeon without good sleep."

"Makes sense," James said. "Let's buy some wood and get a fire going for tonight."

* * *

The party agreed to split up to find the various materials they needed. Stella and James looked for wood and camping supplies.

James didn't mind spending time with Stella, but he found that Stella was a bargain hunter, and in a mining outpost, there were plenty of places selling goods to adventurers like they. He held his tongue as they went through store after after store. But hey, it was time with Stella.

"James, I'm worried about Paul," Stella said.

"Stella, we'll probably never see him again."

"Maybe," Stella said. "I... know something."

"Yeah?"

She paused."I... guess it doesn't matter now, except that it kind of does. The fight he got into with a superior? It was over a Worker girl who said he was stalking her."

"...*What?*" James asked.

"He shouted up and down how it was a false accusation. I thought so, too, for... reasons. The fight got him Transmitted, after all, not the accusation per se. He was always perfectly polite and respectful to me."

"Paul, *polite?*"

Stella paused, as if realizing for herself how strange that was. "Anyway, I think... if he..." She trailed off.

"If Paul is going to try to stalk you, we need to keep a look out," James said. "If I see him again, I'll tell him you're my fiancée, and don't nurcking mess with you."

"...Thanks," Stella said, and looked up at the sky. "We need to get moving. There's two more stores I want to check out..."

* * *

Unfortunately, they could not find incense logs anywhere, not that the rest of the Surface knew what they were. So when they regathered at dusk outside the gates, the fire they started was ordinary.

Lucky refused to cast 'sanctuary.' "With our total _hamartia_, it's going to have no effect. It'll just keep out the smaller monsters, not something big."

"But no smaller monsters is _something,_" James pleaded.

"And if we need that blue scroll to power a healing spell?" Lucky countered.

"She does have a point." Ralph stroked his chin.

"We should just get used to sleeping in watches every night, no matter what," Violet said. "I don't think we're ever going to get a good night's sleep for the next few months."

"We'll do it in shifts. I'll take first," Lucky said. "Stella, how about you share it with me, then I'll sleep and you share with James?"

James was about to protest that he wanted to stay up with Stella first, but Stella spoke before he could. "Works for me. We can talk about enchanting mechanics."

"Yeah, that makes sense," James begrudgingly admitted.

They sorted out the rest of watches. James lay down and thought he couldn't sleep, but fatigue from sleepless nights soon took him.

* * *

He dreamed of his trial, but the accuser wasn't Vulkan Lisp, it was Paul. But Paul didn't have a legal case, he had a machine to fix, and James couldn't fix it but Paul could. The Arbiter kept ordering it redone, and try as James could, he could never fix it to the Arbiter's satisfaction. And Paul reached for Stella, and she screamed---

His eyes shot open. That wasn't part of the dream!
