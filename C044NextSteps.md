## Next steps

"A key fragment?" Violet asked.

"Yes, and it's already been processed," Lucky put it in her inventory. "Tell no one. We're a third of the way there."

James suddenly realized how insanely generous the captain had been. "I thought the Slaver leaders didn't leave their cities," James whispered.

"Evidently this one did," Ralph said, shaking his head. "Maybe he wanted to worldclimb, so he was off to get more resources and slaves."

"I don't think these guys were after slaves," Violet said, and tapped a fallen sword with her foot.

A worried noise from Stella made James turn: she had her destiny screen out, and both her trees were now colossal. James checked his to find the same.

"The Perfects?" Violet asked.

"Yes, or the fact we just killed plenty of monsters and people," Lucky said. "Sometimes we'd pump everyone nearby full of _hamartia_ so we could send more and bigger monsters."

"Great," James said. "Do you think people will mind if we gather up some loot?"

"Let's," Ralph said. "If we killed it, it's ours. That's the rule."

* * *

It took well into the morning before the caravan was ready to move again, between cleaning up the battlefield, fixing the haulers that had been damaged, and the inevitable need for humans to celebrate the victory. 

They also had time to mourn, briefly. The mercenaries had taken plenty of causalties, and so had the actual employed guards and the merchants. Unfortunately, the young merchant who liked Violet had gotten killed---who knew by what---but Paul had made it. "Last time I'm on one of these caravans," he muttered beside Stella as the haulers moved on.

That had also been a good thing: James's party ended up on one of the haulers itself, riding the unpleasant bumpiness as the caravan made the final stretch. He looked around: Ralph seemed sober, Stella distressed, Violet exhausted, and Lucky looking around with her usual weary expression. James himself felt relieved that they had all made it, especially considering the injuries. They couldn't talk about their next steps, however, because Paul was there.

James didn't like how close Paul wanted to sit next to Stella.

"Where you heading off after this?" Paul asked.

"Probably not another caravan trip, either," Violet said.

"Figured." He streched. "Know some good places in Ironspire to crash. Know some real dungholes, too."

"Hey, man," James said at last. "We're going to do our own thing, OK?"

Paul shrugged, but didn't answer.

The caravan rumbled underneath them.

* * *

James had expected Ironspire to have, well, a _spire._ That was sort of true, in that there was an large peak that had numerous mining shafts into it. But it wasn't really a spire. Nor, according to Stella, was the material they mined really iron---it did not exhibit any of the chemical properties of Earth iron, despite being named the same thing. But still, if you were going to name a mine, Ironspire seemed pretty metal.

"They say those shafts were already there when people came here," Paul said. "Wonder who put them in. The Perfects?"

Violet had the look of someone who was trying her hardest not to speak, and Lucky blanched.

"Anyway, if you change your mind, just head over to the Raw Horse. They don't serve raw horse, by the way. Just their name." He walked off.

"He's too smart for his own good," Lucky muttered.

"I'm... I'm a little nervous around him, too," Stella admitted. "He wasn't like this earlier."

"Let's get going," James said. Everyone got up and followed him.

* * *

The outpost looked like someone had sincerely tried to build a stereotypical fantasy mining outpost, but had run into difficulties that this world did not supply the right materials. Everything was made of smoothstone, and the buildings more ramshackle than quaint. Miners walked to and fro---the mining never ceased. Nor did the fires, for sometimes monsters would wander out of the mines, seeking to destroy.

The inn they found was similarly made of smoothstone, even the interior, and James hoped that the room they rented would not have a bed also made from smoothstone. But it was a wooden bed with green straw, so he relaxed a little.

Stella counted their pay. The quartermaster hadn't been able to raise their base pay from the 70 he had promised them ("stupid corporate reasons" he had muttered) but he gave them all a nice bonus, so together they had around five thousand freebucks. That wasn't rich, but it also wasn't poor.

They had also nabbed weapons from the fallen Slavers, but the armor tended either to have holes from the shadowbats or just the wrong size. James found a glowing pair of boots, however, and he found he could jump pretty far with them.

"Now what?" James asked the question they all had.

"Now we make a bunch more money and buy the other two key fragments."
