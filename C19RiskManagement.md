## Risk Management

Of course. James doubted that Tim had been in on the plot, but why not get him out of the room and sweep it while he wasn't looking? It was too busy around meals to search, but if he was out and about... without his items in his inventory.

He slammed his hand on the chair and cried out. But what could he do? He wasn't legally allowed to own it.

Perhaps. But it was still theft.

* * *

The next morning, he approached the guard's office and strode in. "I'd like to report a theft," James said.

The clerk, a Worker, looked up. "Yes?"

"I had some items under my bed. Someone took them," he said. He knew they knew, even if this clerk didn't.

But the clerk's guilty look peaking out his expression gave James the clue. "Could you give a description of these items?"

"Weapons and armor," James said.

"I'm sorry, but you're a Level one citizen. Level one citizens are not allowed---"

"And you are all thieves!" James shouted.

A female Watcher in armor came out and glared at James. "What is the meaning of this?"

"You stole my items!" James shouted.

"You mean your illegal goods."

"Yes!"

"What did you _think_ would happen if you were caught in them?" she asked icily.

James didn't know how to answer that.

"Let it go. We won't pursue you legally, and you stop complaining."

James didn't answer, but walked out.

* * *

He was still very angry at breakfast, lunch, and whenever he was with others. He couldn't help but lash out at others. But his anger turned cold, and coldness into resolve.

He had to get out of there.

At dinner, around one of the big fires, he cleared his throat. "I have an idea," he announced to his friends.

"Yeah?" Tim asked.

"Let's pool our funds, get some gear, and become Wayfarers."

They looked at him as if he had grown an additional pair of heads.

"Hear me out," James said. "We're just going to be stuck here forever if we do nothing."

"We aren't doing nothing!" a friend told him. "I'm about to reach Level 2!"

"Yes, but what the Level 2s do is play around with their wealth," James said, and found anger slipping out of his voice like a crack in a high-pressure pipe. "Would you believe how much resources they waste in fancy buildings?"

"James," Tim said nervously.

"While we're all slaving here, they're making all the money," James said.

"James," Tim said a little louder. "Please stop."

"What difference does it make what your nurcking cert sheet says?" James demanded, even louder. "It's just a bunch of squiggles." He touched his nose and brought out his stats. "This! _THIS_ is what matters!" he shouted.

A guard came up to him. "That's enough."

"No, it's not enough," James said. "_NOTHING_ here is enough! You're all just remaking the Scale!"

James didn't expect to be grappled, which was why he was unprepared when the guards grabbed him and started dragging him off. He shouted and cursed and yelled at everyone, but by the end of the night he was in a cell.

* * *

The next morning, they brought him before not merely an Arbiter, but a woman with Healer-green hair wearing the white gown of the Supreme Council of the Hold. A council, James bitterly mused, little different from the absolute rulers of the Prefect.

"James Algol," she said. "We let you join out of pity, and yet you have chosen to disobey our rules."

"You stole my items," James said, anger flooding into his voice.

"You agreed to turn them in, and didn't. They ultimately belonged to the Hold when you signed the paper that agreed to sell them to us."

James didn't have a retort to that. At last he said. "Don't any of you care that you're just spinning your wheels in the Underneath?"

"You do not understand. You are interested in climbing the worlds," she said. "We are not. We are interested in managing risks. An expedition to one of the Labyrinths is outside an acceptable risk profile, in addition to providing no useful benefits for the Hold."

"Then why are you down here?" James asked.

"Why are you?" she asked. "None of us consented to be sent here. Not all of us are interested in playing the Perfect's game, whatever this 'game' is."

James fell silent. "But... what about the Trogs?"

"We are not Trogs. We are a Hold. The Trogs engage in risks because they want to, and because the ores they find and mine are valuable enough for us to pay them in bluefruit and other cultivated plants. But they cannot survive without the massive harvests that only a Hold can produce."

"But the Hold could not survive without resources found outside, right?"

"That is correct. Our relationship is symbiotic. But our relationship can only be symbiotic because we are managing the risks. You, James Algol, are currently a risk."

James held his breath.

"You have three options," Dr. Prime said. "You can give up any talk or ambition to climb, and become a well-behaved citizen of the Blue Sunrise Hold. Or you can leave with the next Trog Tribe that comes by. Or you can join a party of Wayfarers that stops by, if one will take you. If you do not choose any of those three, we will expell you, and you can survive on your own. Do you understand me?"

James didn't answer.

"Mr. Algol, do you understand me?" she repeated.

"Yes, ma'am. I'll think about it."

"Think fast. You need to answer within the next month. Dismissed."
